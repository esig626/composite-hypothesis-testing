\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{El{\'i}as Vera-Sig{\"u}enza}\\

\begin{abstract}
We study minimax composite binary hypothesis testing under an exponentially decaying worst case Type I constraint. We derive finite blocklength converse and achievability bounds for the worst case Type II error. The converse reduces every admissible composite test to simple binary subproblems. For achievability, two uniform R\'enyi inequalities yield a single threshold test based on the extended log--likelihood ratio induced by a joint R\'enyi projection of convex weakly compact density classes on a dominated measurable space, allowing supports to vary. Under the stated projection and convergence assumptions, the bounds identify the directed composite Kullback--Leibler separation as the critical rate. For compact convex classes with full support on a finite alphabet, a R\'enyi--Hoeffding saddle point gives the exact minimax Type II error exponent at every subcritical rate, together with the matching polynomial order and logarithmic threshold correction. In the same setting, a fixed uniform Type I level yields the forward directed composite Kullback--Leibler separation as the exact minimax Type II error exponent through the limit of the reverse R\'enyi family as its order tends to zero. We also obtain exact calibration within randomised upper threshold tests based on a fixed projected log--likelihood ratio and a finite blocklength hierarchy separating analytical, threshold, and projection losses. Finally, we give sufficient conditions for exact reduction to a simple binary problem through projected ordering and Neyman--Pearson optimality, and through monotone likelihood ratio endpoint structure. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Binary hypothesis testing asks whether observed data were generated under a null hypothesis or an alternative hypothesis. In the Neyman--Pearson (NP) formulation, a test is required to control the Type I error, namely rejection of the null when it is true, while minimising the Type II error, namely acceptance of the null when the alternative is true \cite{neyman1933testing,lehmann2005testing}. When both hypotheses are simple, the NP lemma identifies an optimal likelihood ratio test at every blocklength.

The associated asymptotic tradeoff is also classical. Under a fixed Type I constraint, the Chernoff--Stein lemma identifies the Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence as the optimal Type II error exponent \cite{cover2006elements}. When the Type I error is required to decay exponentially, the tradeoff exhibits a transition between a subcritical Hoeffding regime and a supercritical strong converse regime. The critical rate is governed by the KL divergence from the alternative to the null \cite{blahut1974hypothesis,han2002strong}. Bruno et al.~\cite{bruno2026finite} recently derived finite blocklength R\'enyi achievability and converse bounds for this problem under simple hypotheses and identified the same critical rate. Their subcritical result establishes exponential decay of the Type II error.

In this work, both hypotheses are composite. The null hypothesis is a class of probability laws $\mathcal P$, while the alternative hypothesis is a class $\mathcal Q$. A single test must satisfy the Type I constraint uniformly over $\mathcal P$, and its Type II error is evaluated in the worst case over $\mathcal Q$. The resulting problem is intrinsically minimax. A test designed for one fixed pair may satisfy the required Type I constraint and have small Type II error for that pair, while failing to provide either guarantee uniformly over the full classes.

Composite hypothesis testing has long been studied through robust detection and least favourable distributions. Under suitable ordering or saddle conditions, a least favourable pair reduces the composite minimax problem exactly to a simple binary test \cite{huber1965robust,verdu2003minimax,fauss2021minimax}. Such reductions are powerful, but they require additional structure. Recent work by G\"ul~\cite{gul2026structural} sharpens this distinction by constructing convex, compact uncertainty classes for which one pair maximises every Chernoff functional but fails the convex order condition required for minimax robustness at finite blocklength. Uniform Chernoff optimality alone therefore does not imply least favourability at finite blocklength.

Other lines of work characterise asymptotic exponents for structured
composite problems. Tomamichel and Hayashi
\cite{tomamichel2017operational} consider a fixed null distribution and
structured composite alternatives. They derive the threshold rate, the
optimal error exponent, the strong converse exponent, and second order
asymptotics. For product and Markov alternative classes, these exponents are
expressed through Sibson--$\alpha$ mutual information and R\'enyi
conditional mutual information. Shayevitz~\cite{shayevitz2011renyi}
studies a composite problem with two sensors and relates its optimal worst
case miss detection exponent to a R\'enyi divergence between two
distribution families under a worst noise condition.

In the quantum setting, Berta, Brand\~ao, and Hirche~\cite{berta2021composite} study composite null and alternative hypotheses formed from convex combinations of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) quantum states. Under a fixed Type I constraint, they characterise the asymptotic Type II error exponent by a regularised divergence and conclude that regularisation is necessary in general. These asymptotic and robust testing results do not provide finite blocklength minimax achievability and converse bounds for the classical composite versus composite problem under the exponentially decaying uniform Type I constraint considered here.

Exact asymptotic error exponents for classical composite hypotheses over convex classes are known in broader formulations. See, for example, Mosonyi, Szil\'agyi, and Weiner~\cite{mosonyi2021error}. Our finite alphabet result gives a direct i.i.d. specialisation in the present notation and connects the subcritical exponent to the same joint R\'enyi projection used in the finite blocklength achievability construction.

General minimax theory provides a broad operational framework for composite testing, but it does not directly yield the explicit tensorising Rényi bounds sought here. Our primary objective is therefore to derive finite blocklength achievability and converse bounds for the minimax Type II error under an exponentially decaying Type I constraint that holds uniformly over the null class, without assuming exact reduction to a simple pair. We also treat the fixed Type I regime as a boundary case and identify its exact minimax Type II error exponent in the finite alphabet setting.

The converse and achievability directions are markedly asymmetric. Once a simple binary converse is available, the composite converse follows comparatively directly. Any test satisfying the composite Type I constraint is admissible for every fixed null distribution, while its worst case Type II error dominates the Type II error for every fixed alternative distribution. The simple binary converse can therefore be applied pairwise and then optimised over the two classes.

Achievability, by contrast, is the substantive difficulty. The pairwise reduction employed in the converse argument cannot be reversed because a test designed for one fixed pair need not satisfy the Type I constraint uniformly over the null class or control the Type II error uniformly over the alternative class. We must instead construct a single test for which both guarantees hold uniformly. This is the main technical problem addressed in this work.

\subsection{Contributions}

We establish finite blocklength upper and lower bounds on the minimax Type II error under a Type I constraint that decays exponentially and holds uniformly over the null class. The converse applies a simple binary R\'enyi bound pairwise and then optimises over the two hypothesis classes. For achievability, two uniform R\'enyi inequalities for a single letter statistic yield an explicit threshold test that controls both errors uniformly.

For convex weakly compact density classes on a dominated measurable space, we construct the required statistic as the extended log--likelihood ratio associated with a joint R\'enyi projection. The projection argument allows the supports to vary within the classes and gives the finite alphabet result as a direct special case. Under the stated convergence assumptions, the achievability and converse bounds identify the directed composite Kullback--Leibler separation as the critical rate. Below this rate, the minimax Type II error converges exponentially to zero, whereas above it the error converges exponentially to one.

For compact convex classes with full support on a finite alphabet, we strengthen the subcritical conclusion through a R\'enyi--Hoeffding saddle point. The saddle point shows that the exponent attained by the projected test agrees with the pairwise Hoeffding converse and therefore gives the exact minimax Type II error exponent. Under the same assumptions, we refine this result at first order by identifying the matching polynomial order. The power of the blocklength is determined by the R\'enyi order selected by the saddle point, and the proof also identifies the corresponding logarithmic correction to the projected threshold. 

We also treat a fixed uniform Type I error level under the same finite alphabet assumptions. The small-order limit of the projected reverse Rényi construction gives the exact minimax Type II error exponent as the forward directed composite Kullback–Leibler separation and identifies it as the zero-rate boundary of the composite Hoeffding tradeoff.

We next study direct calibration of the projected log--likelihood ratio. For a fixed R\'enyi order, we minimise the worst case Type II error over randomised upper threshold tests subject to the composite Type I constraint. In the same finite-alphabet setting of compact convex classes with full support, an exactly calibrated rule exists within this fixed family of randomised upper threshold tests based on the projected log--likelihood ratio, exhausts the Type I error budget, and is the unique restricted minimiser as a test function. We then place the calibrated value, the actual error of the closed form projected test, the bound with slack and rejection corrections, and the original exponential bound into a common finite blocklength hierarchy. This hierarchy separates the losses caused by analytical relaxation, threshold selection, and restriction to the projected log--likelihood ratio.

Finally, we treat exact reduction to a simple binary problem as a separate question. We give sufficient projected ordering conditions under which the selected pair attains the worst case errors of the corresponding threshold test. Exact minimax equality follows when this test is also NP optimal for the selected pair. We also establish exact endpoint reduction for separated families with monotone likelihood ratio structure, without requiring the corresponding parametric classes to be convex.

\section{Notation and problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

Let $(\mathcal X,\mathcal F)$ be a measurable space, and let $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ be nonempty classes of probability laws on $(\mathcal X,\mathcal F)$. For $n\geq 1$, let $X^n=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$. For any probability law $P$, write $P^n:=P^{\otimes n}$. The composite versus composite testing problem is
\begin{align*}
    H_{\mathcal P}&:X^n\sim P^n
    \quad\text{for some }P\in\mathcal P,
    \nonumber\\H_{\mathcal Q}
    &:X^n\sim Q^n\quad\text{for some }Q\in\mathcal Q.
%\label{eq:composite-composite-minimax}
\end{align*}
Let
\begin{equation*}
    \mathsf T_n := \left\{\varphi_n:\mathcal X^n\to[0,1]:\varphi_n \text{ is }\mathcal F^{\otimes n}\text{--measurable} \right\}
\end{equation*}
denote the class of randomised tests. For $\varphi_n\in\mathsf T_n$, the value $\varphi_n(x^n)$ is the probability of deciding $H_{\mathcal Q}$ after observing $x^n$. The probability of deciding $H_{\mathcal P}$ is therefore $1-\varphi_n(x^n)$. The corresponding worst case Type I and Type II errors are
\begin{align}
    \alpha_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal P) &:= \sup_{P\in\mathcal P} \mathbb E_{P^n}\!\left[\varphi_n(X^n)\right],  
    \label{eq:composite-errors_1}
    \\ \beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q) &:=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \mathbb E_{Q^n}\!\left[1-\varphi_n(X^n)\right].
\label{eq:composite-errors_2}
\end{align}

We require the worst case Type I error to decay exponentially with rate $r>0$. The corresponding class of admissible tests is
\begin{equation*}
    \Phi_n(r;\mathcal P):=\left\{\varphi_n\in\mathsf T_n: \alpha_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal P)\leq e^{-nr} \right\}.
%\label{eq:composite-admissible-class}
\end{equation*}
The minimax Type II error at blocklength $n$ is
\begin{equation}
    \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q):=\inf_{\varphi_n\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)}
    \beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q).
\label{eq:composite-minimax}
\end{equation}
For fixed probability laws $P$ and $Q$, define the corresponding simple binary value by
\begin{equation*}
\beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(r;P,Q):=\inf_{\substack{\varphi_n\in\mathsf T_n\\\mathbb E_{P^n}[\varphi_n]\leq e^{-nr}}}\mathbb E_{Q^n}[1-\varphi_n].
%\label{eq:simple-binary-minimax-value}
\end{equation*}

For $\lambda>0$, $\lambda\neq 1$, let $\mu$ be any measure dominating $P$ and $Q$, and write
\begin{equation*}
    p:=\frac{dP}{d\mu}, \qquad q:=\frac{dQ}{d\mu}.
\end{equation*}
The order--$\lambda$ Hellinger integral is
\begin{equation}
    Z_\lambda(Q,P):=\int_{\mathcal X}q^\lambda p^{1-\lambda}\,d\mu,
\label{eq:hellinger-integral}
\end{equation}
and the R\'enyi divergence of order $\lambda$ is
\begin{equation}
    D_\lambda(Q\|P):=\frac{1}{\lambda-1}\log Z_\lambda(Q,P).
\label{eq:renyi-divergence-definition}
\end{equation}
These quantities do not depend on the choice of $\mu$. Standard extended
real conventions apply to all divergences and infima. We write $D(Q\|P)$
for the KL divergence. The directed composite R\'enyi separation is
\begin{equation*}
    D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P):= \inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P} D_\lambda(Q\|P),
\end{equation*}
and the corresponding directed composite KL separation is
\begin{equation*}
    D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) :=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P} D(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}

\section{Finite blocklength composite converse}
\label{sec:composite-strong-converse}

We begin by deriving a finite blocklength lower bound on the minimax Type II error under an exponentially decaying worst case Type I constraint. The converse bound holds for arbitrary nonempty classes of probability laws and requires no convexity, compactness, or projection assumptions.

\begin{theorem}[Finite blocklength composite R\'enyi converse]
\label{thm:finite-sample-composite-renyi-converse}

For $r>0$, define
\begin{equation*}
    E_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q):= \sup_{\lambda>1} \frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda} \left[r-D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \right]_+,
\end{equation*}
where $[a]_+:=\max\{a,0\}$ for $a\in\mathbb R$, with $[-\infty]_+:=0$. Then, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
    \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq 1-\exp\left\{ -nE_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \right\}.
\label{eq:finite-sample-composite-renyi-converse}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Fix $\varphi_n\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ and write
\begin{equation*}
    E_{\rm sc}:=E_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
If $E_{\rm sc}=0$, the claim follows from $\beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q)\geq0$. Suppose that $E_{\rm sc}>0$. For any $\delta\in(0,E_{\rm sc})$, choose $\lambda_\delta>1$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\lambda_\delta-1}{\lambda_\delta} \left[ r-D_{\lambda_\delta}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \right]_+ \geq E_{\rm sc}-\delta.
\end{equation*}
Since the right side is positive,
\begin{equation*}
    D_{\lambda_\delta}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)<r<+\infty.
\end{equation*}
Hence, for every $\eta>0$, there exist
$P_\eta\in\mathcal P$ and $Q_\eta\in\mathcal Q$ such that 
\begin{equation*}
    D_{\lambda_\delta}(Q_\eta\|P_\eta) \leq D_{\lambda_\delta}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)+\eta.
\end{equation*}
Since $\varphi_n\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$,
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb E_{P_\eta^n}[\varphi_n] \leq e^{-nr}.
\end{equation*}
Bruno et al.~\cite{bruno2026finite} provide a related simple binary antecedent. Here the required pairwise step follows directly from H\"older's inequality. Since
$D_{\lambda_\delta}(Q_\eta\|P_\eta)<+\infty$ and $\lambda_\delta>1$, we have $Q_\eta\ll P_\eta$, and hence $Q_\eta^n\ll P_\eta^n$. Set $L_{\eta,n}:=\frac{dQ_\eta^n}{dP_\eta^n}$. If $\mathbb E_{P_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]=0$, nonnegativity and absolute continuity give $\mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]=0$. Otherwise, H\"older's inequality, $0\leq\varphi_n\leq1$, the preceding Type I
bound, and tensorisation give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]
&=
\mathbb E_{P_\eta^n}[L_{\eta,n}\varphi_n]
\\
&\leq
\bigl(\mathbb E_{P_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]\bigr)^{
(\lambda_\delta-1)/\lambda_\delta}
\bigl(\mathbb E_{P_\eta^n}[L_{\eta,n}^{\lambda_\delta}]
\bigr)^{1/\lambda_\delta}
\\
&\leq
\exp\left\{
-n\frac{\lambda_\delta-1}{\lambda_\delta}
\left[
r-D_{\lambda_\delta}(Q_\eta\|P_\eta)
\right]
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The displayed rejection bound also holds in the zero expectation case. Combining it with
$\mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]\leq1$ and converting rejection
probability under $Q_\eta^n$ to Type II error yields
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[1-\varphi_n]
&=
1-\mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[\varphi_n]
\\
&
\geq
1-\exp\left\{
-n\frac{\lambda_\delta-1}{\lambda_\delta}
\left[
r-D_{\lambda_\delta}(Q_\eta\|P_\eta)
\right]_+
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The order and pair are chosen only through the preceding
$\delta$- and $\eta$-approximations, so neither extremum is required to be
attained.
Because $Q_\eta\in\mathcal Q$,
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q) \geq \mathbb E_{Q_\eta^n}[1-\varphi_n].
\end{equation*}
Using the choice of $(P_\eta,Q_\eta)$ therefore yields
\begin{align*}
\beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q) \geq
1-\exp\Bigg\{-n&\frac{\lambda_\delta-1}{\lambda_\delta}\\ & \times \left[r-D_{\lambda_\delta}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)-\eta \right]_+ \Bigg\}.
\end{align*}
Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ gives
\begin{align*}
\beta_n(\varphi_n;&\mathcal Q) \\ & \geq 1-\exp\Bigg\{ -n\frac{\lambda_\delta-1}{\lambda_\delta} \left[r-D_{\lambda_\delta}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)\right]_+ \Bigg\}\\ &\geq 1-\exp\left\{-n(E_{\rm sc}-\delta)
\right\}. 
\end{align*}
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q) \geq 1-\exp\left\{-nE_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \right\}.
\end{equation*}
Taking the infimum over $\varphi_n\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ proves
\eqref{eq:finite-sample-composite-renyi-converse}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{lemma}[Limit at order one]
\label{lem:composite-renyi-right-continuity}

Suppose that $D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)<+\infty$ and that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there exist $P_\varepsilon\in\mathcal P$, $Q_\varepsilon\in\mathcal Q$, and $\lambda_\varepsilon>1$ such that
\begin{align*}
    D(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon) &\leq D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)+\varepsilon,\\ D_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon) &<+\infty.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{\lambda\downarrow1} D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) = D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\label{eq:limit_from_above}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Monotonicity in the R\'enyi order gives
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \geq D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P),\qquad \lambda>1.
\end{equation*}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $P_\varepsilon$, $Q_\varepsilon$, and $\lambda_\varepsilon$. Since
\begin{equation*}
D_{\lambda_\varepsilon}(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon)<+\infty,
\end{equation*}
the R\'enyi to KL limit for a fixed pair gives
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow1} D_\lambda(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon) = D(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon)
\end{equation*}
\cite[Theorem~5]{van2014renyi}. Hence, for $\lambda>1$ sufficiently close to one,
\begin{align*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)
&\leq D_\lambda(Q_\varepsilon\|P_\varepsilon)\\
&\leq D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)+2\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation*}
\limsup_{\lambda\downarrow1}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)
\leq D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)+2\varepsilon.
\end{equation*}
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ and combining this with the lower bound proves~\eqref{eq:limit_from_above}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}[Strong converse above the directed KL critical rate]
\label{cor:kl-strong-converse-threshold}

Define the directed composite KL separation by
\begin{equation*}
r_{\rm sc} := D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) = \inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P}D(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}
Suppose that the conditions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:composite-renyi-right-continuity} hold. Then, for every
$r>r_{\rm sc}$, $E_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)>0.$ Consequently, there exists $\gamma>0$ such that, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq 1-e^{-n\gamma}.
\label{eq:composite-strong-converse-exponential}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\longrightarrow1
\end{equation*}
exponentially as $n\to\infty$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By~\eqref{eq:limit_from_above}, there exists $\lambda>1$ such that $D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)<r.$ Therefore,
\begin{equation*}
E_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq \frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda} \left[ r-D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \right] >0.
\end{equation*}
Set $\gamma:=E_{\rm sc}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q).$
The bound~\eqref{eq:composite-strong-converse-exponential} now follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample-composite-renyi-converse}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Projected achievability}
\label{sec:achievability}

The converse bound in Section~\ref{sec:composite-strong-converse} is obtained by testing every admissible composite procedure against fixed binary subproblems. Optimising the resulting R\'enyi lower bound over all pairs $(P,Q)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q$ and over R\'enyi orders $\lambda>1$ gives the finite blocklength strong converse bound. Achievability cannot be obtained by reversing this reduction. To upper bound the minimax value in~\eqref{eq:composite-minimax}, one must construct a single test in $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ and control its Type II error uniformly over $\mathcal Q$. A test designed for one fixed pair may satisfy the Type I constraint under that null distribution and control the Type II error under that alternative distribution, but this is only a pairwise guarantee. It does not by itself control either of the worst case errors in \eqref{eq:composite-errors_1} and~\eqref{eq:composite-errors_2}.

Classical minimax robust testing addresses this problem by identifying least favourable distributions whose likelihood ratio test attains both worst case errors over the uncertainty classes \cite{fauss2021minimax}. Such an exact reduction does not exist in general, even for convex, compact uncertainty classes. Recently, G\"ul~\cite{gul2026structural} demonstrated this by constructing classes for which a unique pair maximises every Chernoff functional but fails the convex order condition required for minimax robustness at finite blocklength. We therefore do not claim that a joint R\'enyi projection yields an operational least favourable pair in general.

Instead, for each $\lambda\in(0,1)$, we select a pair minimising the order--$\lambda$ R\'enyi divergence over $\mathcal Q\times\mathcal P$ and use its extended log--likelihood ratio to construct a single test for the composite problem. The joint optimality of the selected pair yields, through optimisation in each argument, two one-sided Hellinger inequalities, one uniform over $\mathcal Q$ and the other uniform over $\mathcal P$. These inequalities give uniform Type I control over $\mathcal P$ and uniform Type II control over $\mathcal Q$. The resulting construction gives a finite blocklength upper bound on the composite minimax Type II error and establishes exponential achievability below the directed composite KL critical rate.

\subsection{Finite blocklength bound from uniform R\'enyi inequalities}
\label{subsec:uniform-renyi-achievability}

We first derive a conditional finite blocklength bound for any measurable single letter test statistic
\begin{equation*}
h:\mathcal X\to[-\infty,+\infty]
\end{equation*}
satisfying two support conditions and two uniform R\'enyi inequalities. The support conditions ensure that the corresponding $n$--sample statistic is well defined almost surely under the relevant product laws. The null side inequality gives uniform Type I control after tensorisation and Markov's inequality, while the alternative side inequality gives a uniform upper bound on the Type II error.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform R\'enyi achievability bound]
\label{thm:uniform-renyi-achievability}

Fix $0<\lambda<1$, $D\geq0$, $r>0$, and $n\geq1$. Suppose that a measurable single letter test statistic $h:\mathcal X\to[-\infty,+\infty]$ satisfies
\begin{align}
P\{h=+\infty\}&=0 \quad \forall P\in\mathcal P,
\label{eq:abstract-null-infinity}\\
Q\{h=-\infty\}&=0 \quad  \forall Q\in\mathcal Q,
\label{eq:abstract-alternative-infinity}
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}\mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda h}] &\leq e^{(\lambda-1)D}, \nonumber \\ \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_Q[e^{(\lambda-1)h}] &\leq e^{(\lambda-1)D}.
\label{eq:abstract-alternative-moment}
\end{align}

Let $\mathcal M_n$ be the set of sequences containing both a $+\infty$ and a $-\infty$ value of $h$. Define $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n h(X_i)$ outside $\mathcal M_n$ and set $S_n=0$ on $\mathcal M_n$. Set
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min} &:=\frac{n[r-(1-\lambda)D]}{\lambda},\\ \psi_n &:=\mathbf 1\{S_n\geq\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Then $\psi_n\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ and
\begin{equation}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n}[1-\psi_n] \leq\exp\left\{-n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}(D-r)\right\}.
\label{eq:abstract-corrected-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq\min\biggl\{&1-e^{-nr},\\&\exp\left[-n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}(D-r)\right]\biggr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:abstract-global-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The set $\mathcal M_n$ is measurable, and on $\mathcal M_n^c$ the sums of the positive and negative parts of $h$ cannot both be infinite. Hence the stated extension $S_n$ is measurable. Moreover, $\mathcal M_n$ is null under every $P^n$ by~\eqref{eq:abstract-null-infinity} and under every $Q^n$ by~\eqref{eq:abstract-alternative-infinity}. Tonelli's theorem and the product structure therefore give
\begin{align*}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}\mathbb E_{P^n}[e^{\lambda S_n}]&\leq e^{n(\lambda-1)D},\\ \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n}[e^{(\lambda-1)S_n}]&\leq e^{n(\lambda-1)D}.
\end{align*}
For any $\tau\in\mathbb R$, Markov's inequality yields
\begin{equation}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}P^n\{S_n\geq\tau\} \leq e^{-\lambda\tau+n(\lambda-1)D}.
\label{eq:abstract-null-tail}
\end{equation}
The right side is at most $e^{-nr}$ precisely when
$\tau\geq\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}$. On $\{S_n<\tau\}$,
\begin{equation*}
1\leq e^{(1-\lambda)(\tau-S_n)},
\end{equation*}
and hence
\begin{equation}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}Q^n\{S_n<\tau\}\leq e^{(1-\lambda)\tau+n(\lambda-1)D}.
\label{eq:abstract-alternative-tail}
\end{equation}
This bound is increasing in $\tau$. Substituting $\tau=\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}$ gives \eqref{eq:abstract-corrected-upper-bound}. Finally, the randomised test independent of the data $\varphi_n\equiv e^{-nr}$ has Type I error $e^{-nr}$ and Type II error $1-e^{-nr}$. Taking the smaller of the two upper bounds gives \eqref{eq:abstract-global-upper-bound}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Dominated measurable-space R\'enyi projection}
\label{sec:dominated-measurable-projection}

We now give sufficient conditions under which the statistic required by
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-renyi-achievability} exists. Let $\mu$ be a $\sigma$-finite measure. Since every probability density with respect to $\mu$
is a nonnegative element of $L^1(\mu)$ with unit norm, $L^1(\mu)$ is the natural ambient space. Write
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf D_\mu := \left\{ f\in L^1(\mu): f\geq0,\ \int f\,d\mu=1\right\}
\end{equation*}
for the set of probability densities with respect to $\mu$. For $p,q\in\mathsf D_\mu$, let $P$ and $Q$ denote the corresponding probability laws and write
\begin{equation*}
Z_\lambda(q,p):=Z_\lambda(Q,P),
\end{equation*}
where the law-level Hellinger integral is defined in~\eqref{eq:hellinger-integral}. General projection theorems for R\'enyi divergence are often formulated on $\alpha$--convex classes, which are closed under normalised $\alpha$--power mixtures~\cite{kumar2016projection}. The present argument instead retains ordinary linear convexity and, for $0<\lambda<1$, works directly with the joint concavity of the Hellinger integral.

For $0<\lambda<1$, the weighted geometric mean is jointly concave. The inequality
\begin{equation*}
|u^a-v^a|\leq |u-v|^a, \qquad 0<a<1,
\end{equation*}
together with H\"older's inequality shows that $Z_\lambda$ is continuous
in the $L^1$ norm. See also the total variation continuity results for
R\'enyi divergence in~\cite[Theorem~17]{van2014renyi}. Its superlevel sets
are convex and norm closed, hence weakly closed. Thus $Z_\lambda$ is weakly
upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum on
$\mathscr Q\times\mathscr P$ whenever
$\mathscr P,\mathscr Q\subset\mathsf D_\mu$ are nonempty and weakly compact
in $L^1(\mu)$. A maximising density may nevertheless vanish on a set of positive measure. The following lemma justifies differentiation along a feasible segment without assuming common support. It also gives the support restriction and integral inequality needed in the projection argument.

\begin{lemma}[Complete feasible one-sided derivative]
\label{lem:complete-feasible-directional-derivative}

Let $(\mathcal Y,\mathcal G,\mu)$ be a measure space, let
$0<\alpha<1$, and let $w,x,y:\mathcal Y\to[0,\infty)$ be measurable and
finite $\mu$-almost everywhere. For $0\leq t\leq1$, define
\begin{equation*}
F(u):=\int wu^\alpha\,d\mu, \quad y_t:=(1-t)y+tx.
\end{equation*}
Suppose that
\begin{equation*}
0<F(y)<+\infty
\end{equation*}
and that, for every $0\leq t\leq1$,
\begin{equation*}
F(y_t)\leq F(y).
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\mu\bigl(\{w>0,\ y=0,\ x>0\}\bigr)=0.
\label{eq:directional-no-new-support}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation*}
g:= \begin{cases}
wy^{\alpha-1}x, & y>0,\\
0, & y=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Then $g\in L^1(\mu)$ and
\begin{equation}
\int g\,d\mu=\int_{\{y>0\}}wy^{\alpha-1}x\,d\mu \leq F(y).
\label{eq:directional-moment}
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
\lim_{t\downarrow0} \frac{F(y_t)-F(y)}{t}=\alpha\left[\int_{\{y>0\}}wy^{\alpha-1}x\,d\mu-F(y) \right]\leq0.
\label{eq:directional-derivative}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:directional-derivative}. We now
apply the lemma separately to the two coordinates of a joint maximiser.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform R\'enyi bounds from a dominated projection]
\label{thm:dominated-projection-uniform-renyi-inequalities}

Fix $0<\lambda<1$. Let
$\mathscr P,\mathscr Q\subset\mathsf D_\mu$ be nonempty, convex, and weakly
compact in $L^1(\mu)$, and let $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ denote the
corresponding classes of probability laws. Choose
\begin{equation*}
(q_\lambda^\star,p_\lambda^\star)
\in
\operatorname*{argmax}_{(q,p)\in\mathscr Q\times\mathscr P}
Z_\lambda(q,p)
\end{equation*}
and set
\begin{equation*}
z_\lambda^\star:=Z_\lambda(q_\lambda^\star,p_\lambda^\star).
\end{equation*}
Assume that $z_\lambda^\star>0$. Let $P_\lambda^\star$ and
$Q_\lambda^\star$ be the probability laws with densities
$p_\lambda^\star$ and $q_\lambda^\star$, respectively. Assume also that
the sum measure $P_\lambda^\star+Q_\lambda^\star$ dominates every law
in both classes, that is,
\begin{equation}
R\ll P_\lambda^\star+Q_\lambda^\star
\qquad
\text{for every }R\in\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q.
\label{eq:selected-pair-domination}
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation*}
\xi:=P_\lambda^\star+Q_\lambda^\star.
\end{equation*}
Choose versions
\begin{equation*}
a:=\frac{dP_\lambda^\star}{d\xi},
\qquad
b:=\frac{dQ_\lambda^\star}{d\xi}
\end{equation*}
such that, on $\{p_\lambda^\star+q_\lambda^\star>0\}$,
\begin{equation*}
a=\frac{p_\lambda^\star}
        {p_\lambda^\star+q_\lambda^\star},
\qquad
b=\frac{q_\lambda^\star}
        {p_\lambda^\star+q_\lambda^\star},
\end{equation*}
and set $a=b=0$ elsewhere. Define the extended log--likelihood ratio
\begin{equation*}
h_\lambda^\star:=\begin{cases}
\log(b/a), & a>0,\ b>0,\\
-\infty, & a>0,\ b=0,\\
+\infty, & a=0,\ b>0,\\
0, & a=b=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{align}
P\{h_\lambda^\star=+\infty\}
&=0 \quad \forall P\in\mathcal P,
\label{eq:dominated-null-infinity}\\
Q\{h_\lambda^\star=-\infty\}
&=0 \quad \forall Q\in\mathcal Q,
\label{eq:dominated-alternative-infinity}
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[e^{\lambda h_\lambda^\star}\right]
&\leq z_\lambda^\star,
\label{eq:dominated-null-moment}\\
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_Q\!\left[e^{(\lambda-1)h_\lambda^\star}\right]
&\leq z_\lambda^\star.
\label{eq:dominated-alternative-moment}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)
=
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P),
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
    z_\lambda^\star=e^{(\lambda-1)D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Joint optimality implies optimality in each coordinate. Define
\begin{equation*}
C_\lambda^\star
:=
\{p_\lambda^\star>0,\ q_\lambda^\star>0\}.
\end{equation*}

Fix $q\in\mathscr Q$. Convexity of $\mathscr Q$ gives, for $0\leq t\leq1$
\begin{equation*}
Z_\lambda\bigl(
(1-t)q_\lambda^\star+tq,
p_\lambda^\star
\bigr)
\leq z_\lambda^\star.
\end{equation*}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:complete-feasible-directional-derivative} with
\begin{equation*}
\alpha=\lambda,
\qquad
w=(p_\lambda^\star)^{1-\lambda},
\qquad
y=q_\lambda^\star,
\qquad
x=q.
\end{equation*}
It follows that
\begin{equation}
\mu\bigl(
\{p_\lambda^\star>0,\ q_\lambda^\star=0,\ q>0\}
\bigr)=0
\label{eq:dominated-alternative-support}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation*}
\int_{C_\lambda^\star} q\,(q_\lambda^\star)^{\lambda-1} (p_\lambda^\star)^{1-\lambda}\,d\mu \leq z_\lambda^\star.
\end{equation*}

Similarly, fix $p\in\mathscr P$ and apply the lemma along $(1-t)p_\lambda^\star+tp$ with
\begin{equation*}
\alpha=1-\lambda, \quad w=(q_\lambda^\star)^\lambda, \quad y=p_\lambda^\star, \quad x=p.
\end{equation*}
This gives
\begin{equation}
\mu\bigl( \{q_\lambda^\star>0,\ p_\lambda^\star=0,\ p>0\} \bigr)=0
\label{eq:dominated-null-support}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation*}
\int_{C_\lambda^\star} p\,(q_\lambda^\star)^\lambda (p_\lambda^\star)^{-\lambda}\,d\mu \leq z_\lambda^\star.
\end{equation*}

Set
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
A_\lambda^\star
&:=\{p_\lambda^\star>0,\ q_\lambda^\star=0\},\\
B_\lambda^\star
&:=\{p_\lambda^\star=0,\ q_\lambda^\star>0\},\\
N_\lambda^\star
&:=\{p_\lambda^\star=0,\ q_\lambda^\star=0\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Equation~\eqref{eq:dominated-alternative-support} implies that $Q(A_\lambda^\star)=0$ for every $Q\in\mathcal Q$, while \eqref{eq:dominated-null-support} implies that $P(B_\lambda^\star)=0$ for every $P\in\mathcal P$. The domination condition \eqref{eq:selected-pair-domination} gives
\begin{equation*}
R(N_\lambda^\star)=0
\qquad \text{for every }R\in\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q.
\end{equation*}
The definition of $h_\lambda^\star$ therefore yields
\eqref{eq:dominated-null-infinity} and
\eqref{eq:dominated-alternative-infinity}.

The same support relations give
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[e^{\lambda h_\lambda^\star}\right]
&=
\int_{C_\lambda^\star}
p\,(q_\lambda^\star)^\lambda
(p_\lambda^\star)^{-\lambda}\,d\mu
\leq z_\lambda^\star,\\
\mathbb E_Q\!\left[e^{(\lambda-1)h_\lambda^\star}\right]
&=
\int_{C_\lambda^\star}
q\,(q_\lambda^\star)^{\lambda-1}
(p_\lambda^\star)^{1-\lambda}\,d\mu
\leq z_\lambda^\star.
\end{align*}
Taking the respective suprema proves
\eqref{eq:dominated-null-moment} and
\eqref{eq:dominated-alternative-moment}.

Finally, since $\lambda-1<0$, maximising $Z_\lambda$ is equivalent to
minimising $D_\lambda$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)
=
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
Equation~\eqref{eq:renyi-divergence-definition} then gives
\begin{equation*}
z_\lambda^\star
=
e^{(\lambda-1)
D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)}.
\end{equation*}
\end{IEEEproof}

The preceding theorem verifies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-renyi-achievability} with
\begin{equation*}
D=D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
The resulting finite blocklength achievability bound is stated next.

\begin{corollary}[Projected finite blocklength achievability]
\label{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound}

Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:dominated-projection-uniform-renyi-inequalities}, define
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda^\star:=D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)=D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
For $n\geq1$, define
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_{n,\lambda}^\star :={}& \left\{ x^n\in\mathcal X^n: \max_{1\leq i\leq n} h_\lambda^\star(x_i)=+\infty \right\}\\ &\cap \left\{x^n\in\mathcal X^n: \min_{1\leq i\leq n} h_\lambda^\star(x_i)=-\infty\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Set
\begin{equation*}
S_{n,\lambda}^\star(x^n):=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^n h_\lambda^\star(x_i),
&x^n\notin\mathcal M_{n,\lambda}^\star,\\[1ex] 0,
&x^n\in\mathcal M_{n,\lambda}^\star.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
For $\tau\in\mathbb R$, define 
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{n,\tau}^{\lambda,\star}(x^n):= \mathbbm 1
\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star(x^n)\geq\tau\}.
\end{equation*}

For every $r>0$ and every threshold satisfying
\begin{equation}
\tau
\geq
\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}
:=
\frac{n[r-(1-\lambda)D_\lambda^\star]}{\lambda},
\label{eq:projected-minimal-threshold}
\end{equation}
the test $\psi_{n,\tau}^{\lambda,\star}$ belongs to
$\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}
\!\left[
1-\psi_{n,\tau}^{\lambda,\star}
\right]
\leq
\exp\left\{
(1-\lambda)\tau
+
n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star
\right\}.
\label{eq:projected-threshold-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, at $\tau=\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq
\exp\left\{
-n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
(D_\lambda^\star-r)
\right\}.
\label{eq:projected-minimal-threshold-bound}
\end{equation}
Combining this test with the constant randomised test gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq \min\Bigg\{& 1-e^{-nr},\\ &\exp\left[
-n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}(D_\lambda^\star-r) \right] \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The second term decays exponentially in $n$ whenever $r<D_\lambda^\star$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:dominated-projection-uniform-renyi-inequalities} establishes \eqref{eq:abstract-null-infinity}--\eqref{eq:abstract-alternative-moment} with $D=D_\lambda^\star$. Equation~\eqref{eq:abstract-null-tail} shows that every threshold satisfying \eqref{eq:projected-minimal-threshold} gives a test in $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$, while \eqref{eq:abstract-alternative-tail} gives \eqref{eq:projected-threshold-bound}. Substituting $\tau=\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}$ gives \eqref{eq:projected-minimal-threshold-bound}. The global bound follows by also considering the constant randomised test $\varphi_n\equiv e^{-nr}$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Finite alphabet specialisation}
\label{subsec:finite-alphabet-specialisation}

The dominated projection result has the following immediate finite alphabet
specialisation.

\begin{corollary}[Finite alphabet projected bound]
\label{cor:finite-alphabet-projected-bound}

Let $\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and let
$\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\subseteq\Delta_d$ be nonempty, compact, and convex.
Assume that every distribution in $\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q$ has full
support. Then, for every $0<\lambda<1$, there exists
\begin{equation*}
(P_\lambda^\star,Q_\lambda^\star)
\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q
\end{equation*}
such that
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q_\lambda^\star\|P_\lambda^\star)
=
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
The log--likelihood ratio
\begin{equation*}
h_\lambda^\star(x)
:=
\log
\frac{Q_\lambda^\star(x)}
     {P_\lambda^\star(x)}
\end{equation*}
satisfies the support conditions and the two uniform R\'enyi inequalities in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-renyi-achievability}. Consequently, all
conclusions of Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} hold. In particular, for every $n\geq1$ and $r>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq \min\Bigg\{& 1-e^{-nr},\\ & \exp\left[ -n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\bigl\{ D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)-r\bigr\}\right] \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Take $\mu$ to be counting measure on $\mathcal X$. Since the simplex is
finite dimensional, compactness implies norm compactness and hence weak
compactness in $L^1(\mu)$. Convexity supplies the feasible segments required
by the projection argument. Full support implies that every joint maximiser has a strictly positive order--$\lambda$ Hellinger integral and that
\begin{equation*}
R\ll P_\lambda^\star+Q_\lambda^\star
\qquad
\text{for every }R\in\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q.
\end{equation*}
Theorem~\ref{thm:dominated-projection-uniform-renyi-inequalities} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} therefore apply.
\end{IEEEproof}


\subsection{Directed KL critical rate}
\label{subsec:directed-kl-threshold}

The fixed-order projected bound decays whenever
\begin{equation*}
r<D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
To identify the full range of rates obtained by orders below one, we first
establish convergence of the directed composite R\'enyi separations.

\begin{lemma}[Convergence of projected minimum values]
\label{lem:dominated-renyi-minimum-to-kl}

Let $\mathscr P,\mathscr Q\subset\mathsf D_\mu$ be nonempty and weakly
compact in $L^1(\mu)$, and let $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ denote the
corresponding classes of probability laws. Define, for $0<\lambda<1$
\begin{equation*}
d_\lambda:=D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)=\min_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal Q\\P\in\mathcal P}}D_\lambda(Q\|P),
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
d_1:=D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)=\min_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal Q\\P\in\mathcal P}}D(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
d_\lambda\uparrow d_1 \ {\rm as} \ \lambda\uparrow1.
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
The proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:renyi-kl-minimum}.

\begin{corollary}[Directed KL critical rate]
\label{cor:achievability-strong-converse-thresholds-coincide}

Let $\mathscr P,\mathscr Q\subset\mathsf D_\mu$ be nonempty, convex, and weakly compact in $L^1(\mu)$, and let $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ denote the corresponding law classes. Define
\begin{equation*}
r_{\rm crit}:=D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)=\min_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal Q\\P\in\mathcal P}} D(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}
Suppose that there exists a set $\Lambda\subset(0,1)$ with $\sup\Lambda=1$ such that, for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$, a joint maximiser in
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname*{argmax}_{(q,p)\in\mathscr Q\times\mathscr P} Z_\lambda(q,p)
\end{equation*}
can be chosen with positive Hellinger integral and such that
\eqref{eq:selected-pair-domination} holds. Then, for every
$0<r<r_{\rm crit}$, there exist $\lambda\in\Lambda$ and
\begin{equation*}
\gamma_-(r) := \frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda} \left[ D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)-r \right] >0
\end{equation*}
such that, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \leq e^{-n\gamma_-(r)}.
\label{eq:subcritical-projected-exponential}
\end{equation}

If, in addition, $r_{\rm crit}<+\infty$ and the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:composite-renyi-right-continuity} hold, then, for every $r>r_{\rm crit}$, there exists $\gamma_+(r)>0$ such that, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq 1-e^{-n\gamma_+(r)}.
\label{eq:supercritical-converse-exponential}
\end{equation}
No limiting value at $r=r_{\rm crit}$ is asserted. Under the assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-alphabet-projected-bound}, both conclusions hold with $\Lambda=(0,1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:dominated-renyi-minimum-to-kl},
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \uparrow r_{\rm crit} \ \text{as }\lambda\uparrow1.
\end{equation*}
Since $\sup\Lambda=1$, for every $r<r_{\rm crit}$ there exists $\lambda\in\Lambda$ such that
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)>r.
\end{equation*}
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} then gives \eqref{eq:subcritical-projected-exponential}.

Under the additional right-continuity assumptions, the supercritical bound \eqref{eq:supercritical-converse-exponential} follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:kl-strong-converse-threshold}. Finally, under the finite alphabet assumptions, full support ensures that every joint maximiser has positive Hellinger integral and that \eqref{eq:selected-pair-domination} holds for every $0<\lambda<1$. It also makes $D_\lambda(Q\|P)<+\infty$ for every fixed pair and every $\lambda>1$, so the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:composite-renyi-right-continuity} hold.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Exact Type II error exponent on a finite alphabet}
\label{subsec:exact-composite-hoeffding-exponent}

The preceding results identify the directed composite KL divergence as the critical rate. We now strengthen the subcritical conclusion on a finite alphabet by identifying the exact minimax Type II error exponent. The main step is a minimax interchange for the R\'enyi--Hoeffding functional. After reparametrising the R\'enyi order, the functional is concave in the new order parameter; for each fixed order below one, it is jointly convex in the pair of distributions.

This combination of convexity and concavity is related to, but distinct from, the $\alpha$-convex projection geometry studied by Kumar and Sason~\cite{kumar2016projection}. Their framework fixes the R\'enyi order and changes the geometry of the feasible class through normalised $\alpha$-power mixtures. This yields projection and Pythagorean properties that need not hold for classes that are convex only under ordinary linear mixtures. Here the hypothesis classes remain convex under ordinary mixtures. For $0<\lambda<1$, the R\'enyi divergence $D_\lambda(Q\|P)$ is jointly convex in $(P,Q)$, while the change of variables
\begin{equation*}
\rho=\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
\end{equation*}
and the Hoeffding variational representation make the exponent functional concave in $\rho$. Thus the present reparametrisation produces a minimax problem that is convex in the distribution pair and concave in the order parameter. Sion's minimax theorem then yields a R\'enyi--Hoeffding saddle point. For $0<\lambda<1$, define
\begin{equation}
F_r(\lambda;P,Q) := \frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda} \left[D_\lambda(Q\|P)-r\right].
\label{eq:renyi-hoeffding-functional}
\end{equation}
We first record precisely the simple pair input, in the notation of \eqref{eq:renyi-hoeffding-functional}, used below. For full support laws $P,Q$ on a finite alphabet of size $d$ and $0<r<D(Q\|P)$,
\begin{align*}
    \lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n} \log \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(r;P,Q)=\max_{0<s<1}F_r(s;P,Q).
\end{align*}
Under the identification
\begin{equation*}
    (\varrho,\sigma,T)=(Q,P,\varphi),
\end{equation*}
the Type I error in Mosonyi, Szil\'agyi, and Weiner~\cite[Lem.~II.25]{mosonyi2021error} corresponds to our Type II error, while their Type II error corresponds to our Type I error. Their Hoeffding divergence therefore takes the form
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{0<s<1}\frac{1-s}{s}\left[D_s(Q\|P)-r\right].
\end{equation*}
Their formulation permits randomised tests and states achievability through asymptotic exponent pairs. We next verify that the same exponent holds when the constraint
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{P^n}[\varphi_n]\leq e^{-nr}
\end{equation*}
is imposed for every $n$. Set
\begin{equation*}
    \psi(s) := \log Z_s(Q,P), \quad R_s(x) :=\frac{Q(x)^sP(x)^{1-s}}{\exp\{\psi(s)\}}.
\end{equation*}
The likelihood ratio test that decides $Q$ when
\begin{equation*}
    \log\frac{Q^n(X^n)}{P^n(X^n)} \geq n\frac{\psi(s)+r}{s}
\end{equation*}
has Type I error at most $e^{-nr}$ by Markov's inequality and Type II error at most
\begin{equation*}
    \exp\left\{ -n\frac{-\psi(s)-(1-s)r}{s}\right\} = e^{-nF_r(s;P,Q)}.
\end{equation*}
This proves achievability under the exact Type I constraint. For the converse, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds} D(R_s\|P) = s\,\operatorname{Var}_{R_s} \left( \log\frac{Q}{P} \right) > 0,
\end{equation*}
because $P\ne Q$. Hence there is a unique $s_r\in(0,1)$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    D(R_{s_r}\|P)=r.
\end{equation*}
Moreover,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm ds}F_r(s;P,Q) &= \frac{r-D(R_s\|P)}{s^2}, \\ F_r(s_r;P,Q) &= D(R_{s_r}\|Q).
\end{align*}
Thus $s_r$ maximises $F_r(\,\cdot\,;P,Q)$. Choose $t<s_r$, let $t\uparrow s_r$, set $V:=R_t$, and choose $n$-types $V_n\to V$. Symmetrise any admissible randomised test over coordinate permutations. This leaves both product law errors unchanged. Let $c_n(\varphi_n)$ denote its constant rejection probability on the type class $\mathcal T_{V_n}$. The standard finite alphabet type bounds give
\begin{equation*}
    P^n(\mathcal T_{V_n}) \geq (n+1)^{-d}e^{-nD(V_n\|P)}
\end{equation*}
and the analogous inequality under $Q^n$. Since $D(V\|P)<r$, admissibility implies
\begin{equation*}
    c_n(\varphi_n) \leq (n+1)^d e^{-n\{r-D(V_n\|P)\}} =: u_n \longrightarrow 0.
\end{equation*}
Therefore, for all sufficiently large $n$, every admissible test satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb E_{Q^n}[1-\varphi_n] \geq (1-u_n)(n+1)^{-d}e^{-nD(V_n\|Q)}.
\end{equation*}
Taking the infimum over all admissible tests gives
\begin{equation*}
    \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(r;P,Q) \geq (1-u_n)(n+1)^{-d}e^{-nD(V_n\|Q)}.
\end{equation*}
Letting first $n\to\infty$ and then $t\uparrow s_r$ gives the exponent upper bound $D(R_{s_r}\|Q)$, which matches the preceding construction. This proves the exponent formula for randomised tests under the exact Type I constraint. No distinction between lattice and nonlattice cases is needed at first order. The same exponent is also contained in Hoeffding's original multinomial theorem~\cite{hoeffding1965asymptotically}.

\begin{theorem}[R\'enyi--Hoeffding saddle point and exact Type II error exponent]
\label{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle}

Let $\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and let $\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\subseteq\Delta_d$ be nonempty, compact, and convex. Assume that every distribution in $\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q$ has full support. Fix
\begin{equation}
0<r<D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\label{eq:subcritical-hoeffding-rate}
\end{equation}
Then there exist
\begin{equation*}
0<\lambda_r^\star<1, \qquad P_r^\star\in\mathcal P,\qquad Q_r^\star\in\mathcal Q
\end{equation*}
such that
\begin{equation}
F_r(\lambda;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star) \leq F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star) \leq F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P,Q)
\label{eq:renyi-hoeffding-saddle-inequalities}
\end{equation}
for every $0<\lambda<1$, $P\in\mathcal P$, and
$Q\in\mathcal Q$.

Moreover, the exact minimax Type II error exponent exists and is given by
\begin{align}
E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
&:=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
-\frac{1}{n}
\log
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\label{eq:exact-composite-hoeffding-exponent}
\\
&=
\max_{0<\lambda<1}
\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
F_r(\lambda;P,Q)
\nonumber\\
&=
\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
\max_{0<\lambda<1}
F_r(\lambda;P,Q)
\nonumber\\
&=
F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star).
\nonumber
\end{align}

Define the tilted distribution
\begin{equation}
R_r^\star(x)
:=
\frac{
\bigl(Q_r^\star(x)\bigr)^{\lambda_r^\star}
\bigl(P_r^\star(x)\bigr)^{1-\lambda_r^\star}
}{
Z_{\lambda_r^\star}(Q_r^\star,P_r^\star)
},
\qquad
x\in\mathcal X.
\label{eq:hoeffding-saddle-tilted-law}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
D(R_r^\star\|P_r^\star)
&=
r,
\label{eq:hoeffding-saddle-rate-matching}\\
E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
&=
D(R_r^\star\|Q_r^\star).
\label{eq:hoeffding-saddle-exponent-tilt}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Introduce
\begin{equation*}
\rho:=\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda},
\qquad
\lambda_\rho:=\frac{1}{1+\rho},
\end{equation*}
and, for $\rho>0$, define
\begin{equation*}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
:=
F_r(\lambda_\rho;P,Q)
=
\rho
\left[
D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q\|P)-r
\right].
\end{equation*}
Set $G_r(0;P,Q):=0$.

For $0<\lambda<1$, define
\begin{equation*}
R_{\lambda;P,Q}(x)
:=
\frac{
Q(x)^\lambda P(x)^{1-\lambda}
}{
Z_\lambda(Q,P)
}.
\end{equation*}
For every $V\in\Delta_d$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&(1-\lambda)D(V\|P)+\lambda D(V\|Q)
\\
&\qquad =
D(V\|R_{\lambda;P,Q})
-
\log Z_\lambda(Q,P).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
-\log Z_\lambda(Q,P)
=
\min_{V\in\Delta_d}
\left\{
(1-\lambda)D(V\|P)
+
\lambda D(V\|Q)
\right\}.
\end{equation*}
Multiplying by $1/\lambda_\rho=1+\rho$ gives
\begin{equation}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
=
\min_{V\in\Delta_d}
\left\{
D(V\|Q)
+
\rho\left[D(V\|P)-r\right]
\right\}.
\label{eq:rho-hoeffding-variational-form}
\end{equation}
The identity also holds at $\rho=0$, because
$\min_VD(V\|Q)=0$.

For fixed $(P,Q)$, the right side of
\eqref{eq:rho-hoeffding-variational-form} is an infimum of affine
functions of $\rho$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\rho\longmapsto G_r(\rho;P,Q)
\end{equation*}
is concave on $[0,\infty)$.

For fixed $\rho>0$,
\begin{equation*}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
=
\rho D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q\|P)-\rho r.
\end{equation*}
Let
\begin{equation*}
(P_\theta,Q_\theta)
:=
\theta(P_0,Q_0)
+
(1-\theta)(P_1,Q_1),
\qquad
0\leq\theta\leq1.
\end{equation*}
Joint concavity of the Hellinger integral gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_\theta,P_\theta)
\geq{}&
\theta Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_0,P_0)
\\
&+
(1-\theta)
Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_1,P_1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Since
\begin{equation*}
u\longmapsto
-\frac{1}{1-\lambda_\rho}\log u
\end{equation*}
is convex and decreasing,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_\theta\|P_\theta)
&\leq
-\frac{1}{1-\lambda_\rho}
\log\Bigl[
\theta Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_0,P_0)
\\
&\hspace{5em}
+
(1-\theta)
Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_1,P_1)
\Bigr]
\\
&\leq
\theta D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_0\|P_0)
\\
&\quad+
(1-\theta)
D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q_1\|P_1).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus $D_{\lambda_\rho}(Q\|P)$ is jointly convex, consistently with
\cite[Th.~11]{van2014renyi}. Therefore
$(P,Q)\mapsto G_r(\rho;P,Q)$ is jointly convex. At $\rho=0$, it is
identically zero and is also convex.

Since $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal Q$ are compact and all their members have
full support, there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that
\begin{equation*}
P(x)\geq\varepsilon,
\qquad
Q(x)\geq\varepsilon
\end{equation*}
for every $x$, $P\in\mathcal P$, and $Q\in\mathcal Q$. For
$0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation*}
0\leq D_\lambda(Q\|P)
\leq D(Q\|P)
\leq\log(1/\varepsilon).
\end{equation*}
It follows that
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
\left|G_r(\rho;P,Q)\right|
\leq
\rho\left[\log(1/\varepsilon)+r\right]
\longrightarrow0
\end{equation*}
as $\rho\downarrow0$. Thus the extension at zero is uniform over the pair
class. The finite-sum expression is continuous away from zero, so $G_r$ is
jointly continuous on
$[0,L]\times\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q$ for every finite $L$.

Define
\begin{equation*}
M:=
\max_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
D(P\|Q)<+\infty.
\end{equation*}
Choosing $V=P$ in
\eqref{eq:rho-hoeffding-variational-form} gives
\begin{equation*}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
\leq
D(P\|Q)-\rho r
\leq
M-\rho r.
\end{equation*}
Choose one
\begin{equation*}
\overline\rho>\frac{M}{r}.
\end{equation*}
Then $G_r(\rho;P,Q)<0$ for every
$\rho\geq\overline\rho$ and every pair, whereas
$G_r(0;P,Q)=0$. Consequently, both the maximisation of
$G_r(\rho;P,Q)$ for each fixed pair and the maximisation of
\begin{equation*}
\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
\end{equation*}
may be restricted to $[0,\overline\rho]$.

On this compact interval, $G_r$ is real-valued, continuous and concave,
hence quasiconcave and upper semicontinuous, in the maximised variable
$\rho$. It is continuous and jointly convex, hence quasiconvex and lower
semicontinuous, in the minimised variable
$(P,Q)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q$. Both domains are compact and convex.
Sion's minimax theorem~\cite[Th.~3.4, p.~174]{sion1958general} therefore
gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{0\leq\rho\leq\overline\rho}
\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
G_r(\rho;P,Q)
=
\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
\max_{0\leq\rho\leq\overline\rho}
G_r(\rho;P,Q).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rho-minimax-interchange}
\end{equation}

Joint continuity and compactness also imply attainment of every extremum in
\eqref{eq:rho-minimax-interchange}. Let
$\rho_r^\star$ maximise the left side and let
$(P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)$ minimise the right side. If the common value is
$v_r$, then
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
v_r
&=
\min_{P,Q}
G_r(\rho_r^\star;P,Q)
\\
&\leq
G_r(\rho_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)
\\
&\leq
\max_\rho
G_r(\rho;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)
=
v_r.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Thus equality holds throughout and
\begin{equation*}
G_r(\rho;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)
\leq
G_r(\rho_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)
\leq
G_r(\rho_r^\star;P,Q)
\end{equation*}
for every $\rho\in[0,\overline\rho]$ and every pair. This deduction uses
attainment but no uniqueness.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:dominated-renyi-minimum-to-kl},
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)
\uparrow
D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)
\qquad
\text{as }\lambda\uparrow1.
\end{equation*}
The strict inequality in
\eqref{eq:subcritical-hoeffding-rate} therefore gives an order
$\lambda<1$ close enough to one that
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)>r.
\end{equation*}
Hence $v_r>0$. Since
\begin{equation*}
\min_{P,Q}G_r(0;P,Q)=0
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\min_{P,Q}G_r(\overline\rho;P,Q)<0,
\end{equation*}
every selected maximiser satisfies
\begin{equation*}
0<\rho_r^\star<\overline\rho.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, the uniform coercive bound makes the selected fixed-pair value
negative for $\rho>\overline\rho$, while its saddle value is positive.
Thus the left saddle inequality extends to all $\rho\geq0$. Setting
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_r^\star
:=
\frac{1}{1+\rho_r^\star}
\end{equation*}
gives \eqref{eq:renyi-hoeffding-saddle-inequalities} for every
$0<\lambda<1$. Positivity also ensures that the extrema in the equivalent
open-order formulas are attained away from $\rho=0$.

The right saddle inequality and the positive factor
$(1-\lambda_r^\star)/\lambda_r^\star$ imply
\begin{equation*}
D_{\lambda_r^\star}(Q_r^\star\|P_r^\star)
=
D_{\lambda_r^\star}(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
The finite alphabet projection argument preceding Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-alphabet-projected-bound} applies to every selected joint minimiser at a fixed order, so it applies to this saddle pair even if the minimiser is not unique. In particular, the projected log--likelihood ratio
\begin{equation*}
h_r^\star(x):=
\log\frac{Q_r^\star(x)}{P_r^\star(x)}
\end{equation*}
yields, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq
\exp\left\{
-nF_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)
\right\}.
\label{eq:saddle-finite-sample-achievability}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
-\frac{1}{n}
\log
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\geq
F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star).
\label{eq:saddle-achievability-exponent}
\end{equation}

Conversely, every test in $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ satisfies the Type I constraint under $P_r^\star$, and the worst case Type II error is no smaller than its error under $Q_r^\star$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star).
\end{equation*}
The selected pair has full support and
\begin{equation*}
D(Q_r^\star\|P_r^\star) \geq D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)> r,
\end{equation*}
so the simple binary exponent formula under the exact Type I constraint applies and gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{n\to\infty} & -\frac{1}{n} \log \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star) \\ &= \max_{0<\lambda<1} F_r(\lambda;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star) \\ &= F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where the last equality is the left saddle inequality. Since $x\mapsto -n^{-1}\log x$ is decreasing, the preceding inequality implies
\begin{equation}
\limsup_{n\to\infty} -\frac{1}{n} \log \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \leq F_r(\lambda_r^\star;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star).
\label{eq:saddle-converse-exponent}
\end{equation}
Combining
\eqref{eq:saddle-achievability-exponent} and \eqref{eq:saddle-converse-exponent} proves \eqref{eq:exact-composite-hoeffding-exponent}.

For any fixed full support pair $(P,Q)$ and $\rho>0$, let
\begin{equation*}
R_\rho(x) := \frac{Q(x)^{\lambda_\rho}P(x)^{1-\lambda_\rho}}{Z_{\lambda_\rho}(Q,P)}.
\end{equation*}
Direct differentiation of the finite-sum expression gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\rho}G_r(\rho;P,Q)=D(R_\rho\|P)-r.
\label{eq:rho-hoeffding-derivative}
\end{equation}
This derivative is taken for a fixed pair before optimisation over $(P,Q)$ and does not require uniqueness.

Apply \eqref{eq:rho-hoeffding-derivative} to
$(P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)$. Since $\rho_r^\star$ is an interior maximiser for this fixed pair, its derivative vanishes. With the law defined in \eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-tilted-law}, this proves \eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-rate-matching}. Substitution into \eqref{eq:rho-hoeffding-variational-form} gives 
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)&=D(R_r^\star\|Q_r^\star)\\&\quad+\rho_r^\star\left[D(R_r^\star\|P_r^\star)-r\right]\\&=D(R_r^\star\|Q_r^\star),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-exponent-tilt}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The preceding theorem gives a direct i.i.d. specialisation, in the present notation, of the broader classical composite exponent results of Mosonyi, Szil\'agyi, and Weiner~\cite{mosonyi2021error}.

\begin{corollary}[Exact subcritical Type II error exponent for simple binary testing]
\label{cor:exact-simple-binary-hoeffding-exponent}

Let $\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and let $P_0$ and $P_1$ be probability distributions with full support on $\mathcal X$. Fix
\begin{equation*}
0<r<D(P_1\|P_0).
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\lim_{n\to\infty} -\frac{1}{n}\log \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(r;P_0,P_1) \\ &\quad= E_{\rm H}^{\mathrm{sp}}(r;P_0,P_1) \\ &\quad:= \max_{0<\lambda<1} \frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda} \left[D_\lambda(P_1\|P_0)-r \right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exact-simple-binary-hoeffding-exponent}
\end{equation}
In particular, there exists $0<\lambda_r^\star<1$ such that
\begin{equation*}
E_{\rm H}^{\mathrm{sp}}(r;P_0,P_1) = \frac{1-\lambda_r^\star}{\lambda_r^\star} \left[ D_{\lambda_r^\star}(P_1\|P_0)-r \right].
\end{equation*}

Define
\begin{equation}
R_r^\star(x) := \frac{P_1(x)^{\lambda_r^\star} P_0(x)^{1-\lambda_r^\star}}{Z_{\lambda_r^\star}(P_1,P_0)}.
\label{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-tilted-law}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
D(R_r^\star\|P_0) &= r,
\label{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-rate-matching}\\
E_{\rm H}^{\mathrm{sp}}(r;P_0,P_1) &= D(R_r^\star\|P_1).
\label{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-tilted-exponent}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle} to the singleton classes
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal P=\{P_0\}, \qquad \mathcal Q=\{P_1\}.
\end{equation*}
The minimisation over $(P,Q)$ is then vacuous, and
\eqref{eq:exact-composite-hoeffding-exponent},
\eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-rate-matching}, and
\eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-exponent-tilt}
give \eqref{eq:exact-simple-binary-hoeffding-exponent},
\eqref{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-rate-matching}, and
\eqref{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-tilted-exponent}, with the specialised
law in \eqref{eq:simple-binary-hoeffding-tilted-law}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-simple-binary-hoeffding-exponent} complements the finite blocklength bounds of Bruno et al.~\cite{bruno2026finite} by identifying the exact subcritical exponent, which is the classical simple binary Hoeffding exponent in the present orientation and notation.

\begin{remark}[Exponent optimality and exact reduction]
\label{rem:exponent-versus-finite-sample-least-favourability}

The saddle point in \eqref{eq:renyi-hoeffding-saddle-inequalities} concerns the minimax exponent only. It does not imply that $(P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)$ attains the worst case Type I and Type II errors of a single test at finite blocklength. Sufficient conditions for exact reduction are given in Section~\ref{sec:exact-closure-projected-score-ordering}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Finite blocklength refinements]
\label{rem:finite-sample-refinements-exact-exponent}

At the saddle order $\lambda_r^\star$, the projected bound in \eqref{eq:saddle-finite-sample-achievability} already has exponent $E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)$. Hence any sharper upper bound $B_n$ satisfying
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \leq B_n \leq e^{-nE_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)}
\end{equation*}
has the same first order exponent.
\end{remark}

Appendix~\ref{app:composite-polynomial-order} gives the corresponding polynomial refinement. For every saddle triple furnished by Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle}, 
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)=\Theta\!\left( n^{-1/(2\lambda_r^\star)} e^{-nE_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)}\right).
\end{equation*}
This $\Theta$ relation determines the polynomial order but does not assert a ratio one limit or a universal leading constant.

\begin{corollary}[Saddle point conditions for affine classes]
\label{cor:affine-class-hoeffding-first-order-characterisation}

Let
\begin{equation*}
P_s=p_0+sa, \qquad Q_t=q_0+tb, \qquad (s,t)\in[0,1]^2,
\end{equation*}
be probability distributions on a finite alphabet, where
\begin{equation*}
\sum_x a(x)=\sum_x b(x)=0.
\end{equation*}
Assume that
\begin{equation*}
P_s(x)>0, \qquad Q_t(x)>0
\end{equation*}
for every symbol $x$ and every $(s,t)\in[0,1]^2$. Define
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal P:=\{P_s:0\leq s\leq1\}, \qquad \mathcal Q:=\{Q_t:0\leq t\leq1\}.
\end{equation*}
Fix
\begin{equation*}
0<r<D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P),
\end{equation*}
and suppose that
\begin{equation*}
0<\lambda^\star<1, \qquad (s^\star,t^\star)\in[0,1]^2.
\end{equation*}
Define
\begin{equation}
R^\star(x) := \frac{Q_{t^\star}(x)^{\lambda^\star} P_{s^\star}(x)^{1-\lambda^\star}}{Z_{\lambda^\star}(Q_{t^\star},P_{s^\star})}.
\label{eq:affine-hoeffding-tilted-law}
\end{equation}
Assume that
\begin{equation}
D(R^\star\|P_{s^\star})=r
\label{eq:affine-hoeffding-rate-matching}
\end{equation}
and that, for every $s,t\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}
\left[-\sum_xR^\star(x)\frac{a(x)}{P_{s^\star}(x)}\right](s-s^\star)\geq 0,
\label{eq:affine-null-first-order-condition}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\left[-\frac{\lambda^\star}{1-\lambda^\star} \sum_x R^\star(x)\frac{b(x)}{Q_{t^\star}(x)}\right](t-t^\star)\geq0.
\label{eq:affine-alternative-first-order-condition}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
F_r(\lambda;P_{s^\star},Q_{t^\star})
\leq
F_r(\lambda^\star;P_{s^\star},Q_{t^\star})
\leq
F_r(\lambda^\star;P_s,Q_t)
\label{eq:affine-hoeffding-saddle}
\end{equation}
for every $0<\lambda<1$ and $(s,t)\in[0,1]^2$. Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{n\to\infty} -\frac1n \log \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) = D(R^\star\|Q_{t^\star}).
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
With $R^\star$ as defined in \eqref{eq:affine-hoeffding-tilted-law}, fix $\lambda^\star$. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial}{\partial s} D_{\lambda^\star}(Q_t\|P_s) &= -\sum_x R_{\lambda^\star,s,t}(x) \frac{a(x)}{P_s(x)}, \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} D_{\lambda^\star}(Q_t\|P_s) &= -\frac{\lambda^\star}{1-\lambda^\star} \sum_x R_{\lambda^\star,s,t}(x) \frac{b(x)}{Q_t(x)},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
R_{\lambda,s,t}(x):=\frac{Q_t(x)^\lambda P_s(x)^{1-\lambda}}{Z_\lambda(Q_t,P_s)}.
\end{equation*}
The map
\begin{equation*}
(s,t)\longmapsto D_{\lambda^\star}(Q_t\|P_s)
\end{equation*}
is convex. Conditions \eqref{eq:affine-null-first-order-condition} and \eqref{eq:affine-alternative-first-order-condition} are the two coordinate components of the joint variational inequality
\begin{equation*}
\nabla D_{\lambda^\star} (Q_{t^\star}\|P_{s^\star}) \mathbin{\cdot} \left((s,t)-(s^\star,t^\star)\right) \geq0
\end{equation*}
on $[0,1]^2$. This formulation includes boundary points: the derivative is nonnegative at a lower endpoint, nonpositive at an upper endpoint, and zero at an interior optimum. Convexity therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
D_{\lambda^\star} (Q_{t^\star}\|P_{s^\star}) \leq D_{\lambda^\star}(Q_t\|P_s)
\end{equation*}
for every $(s,t)\in[0,1]^2$. Set
\begin{equation*}
\rho^\star := \frac{1-\lambda^\star}{\lambda^\star}.
\end{equation*}
Applying the fixed pair derivative in \eqref{eq:rho-hoeffding-derivative} before optimisation over $(s,t)$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\rho} G_r(\rho;P_{s^\star},Q_{t^\star}) = D(R_\rho\|P_{s^\star})-r,
\end{equation*}
where $R_\rho$ is the tilt of
$(P_{s^\star},Q_{t^\star})$ at
$\lambda_\rho=(1+\rho)^{-1}$. Equation
\eqref{eq:affine-hoeffding-rate-matching} therefore makes $\rho^\star$ a stationary point. The fixed-pair function is concave in $\rho$, so $\rho^\star$ is a global maximiser; neither strict concavity nor uniqueness is required. Together with the fixed-order global minimum above, this gives \eqref{eq:affine-hoeffding-saddle}. Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle} and the variational identity give
\begin{align*}
E_{\rm H}(r;&\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \\ & = D(R^\star\|Q_{t^\star}) + \rho^\star \left[D(R^\star\|P_{s^\star})-r\right] = D(R^\star\|Q_{t^\star}),
\end{align*}
which proves the exponent conclusion.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Calibration and finite blocklength gaps for projected log--likelihood ratio tests}
\label{sec:calibrated-projected-tests}

The projected R\'enyi bound gives the closed form threshold $\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}$, whose Type I admissibility follows from the uniform null inequality. A different refinement fixes the projected log--likelihood ratio and calibrates its threshold directly against the composite Type I constraint. This changes the test rather than only its analytical upper bound.

For $\tau\in\mathbb R$ and $0\leq\eta\leq1$, define the randomised projected threshold test
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}(x^n) :={}& \mathbbm 1 \{S_{n,\lambda}^\star(x^n)>\tau\} \\ &+ \eta \mathbbm 1 \{S_{n,\lambda}^\star(x^n)=\tau\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:randomised-projected-threshold-test}
\end{equation}
The deterministic test in Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} corresponds to $\eta=1$.

\subsection{Direct calibration of the projected log--likelihood ratio}
\label{subsec:direct-calibration-projected-llr}

For fixed $0<\lambda<1$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_{n,\lambda}(r)
:=
\Bigl\{
(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb R\times[0,1]:
\alpha_n\!\left(
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star};
\mathcal P
\right)
\leq
e^{-nr}
\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:projected-calibration-feasible-set}
\end{equation}

The restricted minimax Type II value generated by the fixed projected
log--likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}
(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
:=
\inf_{(\tau,\eta)\in\mathcal C_{n,\lambda}(r)}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}
\left[
1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calibrated-projected-value}
\end{equation}
This optimisation is restricted to randomised upper threshold tests based
on $S_{n,\lambda}^\star$ and does not range over the full class
$\mathsf T_n$. The calibration argument requires less structure than the
construction of $S_{n,\lambda}^\star$. For a fixed real valued measurable
statistic with nonempty finite range, nonempty null and alternative classes
and a target Type I level in $(0,1)$ suffice for existence of a maximal
admissible threshold rule and restricted optimality. Convexity is used only
upstream to construct the projected log--likelihood ratio. Compactness and
full support provide the uniform positivity needed for uniqueness of the
optimal test function.

\begin{proposition}[Finite blocklength calibration of the projected log--likelihood ratio]
\label{prop:finite-sample-projected-llr-calibration}
Assume the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-alphabet-projected-bound}. For every $n\geq1$, $r>0$, and $0<\lambda<1$, there are
\begin{equation*}
\tau_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}\in\mathbb R, \qquad \eta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}\in[0,1]
\end{equation*}
such that
\begin{equation}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P} \mathbb E_{P^n}\left[\psi_{n,\tau_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}},\eta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}}^{\lambda,\star}\right]=e^{-nr}.
\label{eq:calibrated-projected-type-one-equality}
\end{equation}
The induced test is the unique maximal admissible test function in the
randomised upper threshold family and the unique restricted minimiser as a
test function. Every optimal pair $(\tau,\eta)$ is a representation of
this same test, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}
(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
=
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}
\left[
1-
\psi_{n,\tau_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}},
\eta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}}^{\lambda,\star}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calibrated-projected-type-two-value}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}
(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q),
\label{eq:minimax-below-calibrated-projected-value}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}
(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\leq
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}
\left[
1-
\psi_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda,\star}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calibrated-below-renyi-threshold-test}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Full support makes $h_\lambda^\star$ real valued, and the finite alphabet
makes $S_{n,\lambda}^\star$ take values in a nonempty finite set. Write its
distinct values as
\begin{equation*}
v_1<\cdots<v_m
\end{equation*}
and, for $1\leq j\leq m$ and $0\leq\eta\leq1$, set
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{j,\eta}
:=
\mathbbm 1\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star>v_j\}
+
\eta\mathbbm 1\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star=v_j\}.
\end{equation*}
These tests form a pointwise chain. Starting from $\psi_{m,0}=0$, traverse
$\eta$ from $0$ to $1$ at $v_m$, then at $v_{m-1}$, and so on, ending at
$\psi_{1,1}=1$. The adjacent intervals join because
\begin{equation}
\psi_{j,1}=\psi_{j-1,0},
\qquad 2\leq j\leq m.
\label{eq:adjacent-projected-boundaries-join}
\end{equation}
Every pair $(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb R\times[0,1]$ induces a test function on
this joined chain.

For each boundary, define its worst case Type I error by
\begin{equation*}
A_j(\eta)
:=
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}
\mathbb E_{P^n}[\psi_{j,\eta}].
\end{equation*}
If $0\leq\eta\leq\eta'\leq1$, then, for every $P\in\mathcal P$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{P^n}[\psi_{j,\eta'}]
-
\mathbb E_{P^n}[\psi_{j,\eta}]
=
(\eta'-\eta)
P^n\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star=v_j\}.
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
    0 \leq A_j(\eta')-A_j(\eta) \leq \eta'-\eta.
    %\label{eq:null-envelope-boundary-lipschitz}
\end{equation*}
Thus the worst case Type I error varies continuously and
nondecreasingly along each boundary segment. Together with
\eqref{eq:adjacent-projected-boundaries-join}, this gives a continuous nondecreasing Type I error function on the entire compact joined chain, with endpoint values $0$ and $1$. Set $\alpha:=e^{-nr}\in(0,1)$. The feasible portion of the chain is nonempty and closed, whereas the test identically equal to one is infeasible. Hence the chain has a maximal feasible member. Continuity implies that its worst case Type I error equals $\alpha$. Denote any threshold representation of this member by
\begin{equation*}       
    (\tau_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}},\eta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}).
\end{equation*}
This proves \eqref{eq:calibrated-projected-type-one-equality}. By
construction, the calibrated test is the maximal admissible member of the pointwise chain. Compactness and full support strengthen this conclusion to uniqueness. They give constants
\begin{equation*}
\epsilon_{\mathcal P} := \min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\x\in\mathcal X}}P(x)>0, \qquad \epsilon_{\mathcal Q} := \min_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal Q\\x\in\mathcal X}}Q(x)>0.
\end{equation*}
Every nonempty boundary of $S_{n,\lambda}^\star$ therefore has probability at least $\epsilon_{\mathcal P}^n$ under every $P^n$ and at least $\epsilon_{\mathcal Q}^n$ under every $Q^n$. More generally, if two distinct members $\psi\prec\psi'$ of the joined threshold chain are compared, then $\psi'-\psi$ is positive on a nonempty subset of $\mathcal X^n$ and has a strictly positive smallest nonzero value. It follows uniformly over
$\mathcal P$ that \begin{equation*}
    \sup_{P\in\mathcal P}\mathbb E_{P^n}[\psi']>\sup_{P\in\mathcal P}\mathbb E_{P^n}[\psi],
\end{equation*}
and uniformly over $\mathcal Q$ that
\begin{equation*}
    \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n}[1-\psi'] <\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n}[1-\psi].
\end{equation*}
Hence the calibrated member is unique as a test function. Every smaller admissible member has strictly larger worst case Type II error, while every larger member violates the Type I constraint. This proves the restricted optimality, its uniqueness as a test function, and \eqref{eq:calibrated-projected-type-two-value}. Without uniform positivity, the same chain construction for a statistic with finite range still yields a maximal calibrated restricted minimiser. However, smaller rules may also be optimal if the worst case Type II error is unchanged over part of the threshold family.

The calibrated test belongs to $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$, so optimisation over all of $\mathsf T_n$ gives \eqref{eq:minimax-below-calibrated-projected-value}. The deterministic test with $(\tau,\eta)=(\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1)$ also belongs to $\mathcal C_{n,\lambda}(r)$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound}. Restricted optimality therefore gives \eqref{eq:calibrated-below-renyi-threshold-test}.
\end{IEEEproof}
Uniqueness in the proposition concerns the induced decision function, not necessarily its parameters. If the calibrated rule randomises strictly inside an attainable boundary, its pair $(\tau,\eta)$ is unique. If it is the deterministic rule rejecting on $\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star\geq v_j\}$ for some $2\leq j\leq m$, then the same rule is represented by $(v_j,1)$, by $(v_{j-1},0)$, and by every $\tau\in(v_{j-1},v_j)$ with arbitrary $\eta\in[0,1]$. These are equivalent representations, not incomparable calibrated tests. The right side of \eqref{eq:calibrated-below-renyi-threshold-test} is the actual worst case
Type II error of the deterministic projected test. It is distinct from the analytical R\'enyi upper bound. Corollary \ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} gives the separate comparison
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}
(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
&\leq
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}
\left[
1-
\psi_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda,\star}
\right]
\\
&\leq
\exp\left[
-n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
\bigl(D_\lambda^\star-r\bigr)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:calibrated-actual-and-analytical-comparison}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Simple binary calibration]
\label{cor:simple-binary-calibrated-projected-llr}
Assume the finite alphabet and full support conditions above, and let
$\mathcal P=\{P_0\}$ and $\mathcal Q=\{P_1\}$. Then, for every
$0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation*}
h_\lambda^\star = \log\frac{dP_1}{dP_0},
\end{equation*}
independently of $\lambda$, and
\begin{equation}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}} (r;\{P_0\},\{P_1\}) = \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(r;P_0,P_1).
\label{eq:simple-binary-calibration-is-np}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The only possible projected pair is $(P_\lambda^\star,Q_\lambda^\star)=(P_0,P_1)$, so the displayed identity for $h_\lambda^\star$ follows from its definition and
\begin{equation*}
S_{n,\lambda}^\star = \log\frac{dP_1^n}{dP_0^n}.
\end{equation*}
The calibrated chain is therefore the family of randomised likelihood ratio tests. Its maximal member with Type I error $e^{-nr}$ is the randomised NP test, which attains the simple binary value in \eqref{eq:simple-binary-calibration-is-np}
\cite[Th.~3.2.1]{lehmann2005testing}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Bruno, Vandenbroucque, and Esposito study the simple binary problem from the outset~\cite{bruno2026finite}. In the randomised formulation used here, calibration recovers the exact NP value, whereas their finite blocklength R\'enyi expressions remain analytical bounds on it.

\begin{remark}[Calibration does not imply exact reduction]
Calibration optimises only over upper threshold tests based on the fixed projected log--likelihood ratio. Therefore, the inequality in \eqref{eq:minimax-below-calibrated-projected-value} can be strict. Later, Section~\ref{sec:exact-closure-projected-score-ordering} gives additional projected ordering and NP conditions that guarantee exact reduction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bounds with slack and rejection corrections}
\label{subsec:directly-admissible-slack-rejection-bound}

The following refinement applies to any projected threshold whose composite Type I admissibility is verified directly. The threshold need not satisfy
\begin{equation*}
\tau\geq\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}.
\end{equation*}

Under the assumptions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound}, every
occurrence below of
$e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}$
denotes the nonnegative measurable version that agrees with the ordinary
product on $\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star>-\infty\}$ and is zero on
$\{S_{n,\lambda}^\star=-\infty\}$. The latter set is $Q^n$-null for every
$Q\in\mathcal Q$. With this convention,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
={}&
e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}
\\
&+
e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
\left(
1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}
\right)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
holds as an identity of extended nonnegative measurable functions.

For $(\tau,\eta)\in\mathbb R\times[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q):=\inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star} \psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star} \right]
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q) := \inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\Bigg\{&e^{n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star}-\mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}\right] \\&+\mathbb E_{Q^n}\left[e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star} \psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star} \right] \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\begin{proposition}[Projected bound with slack and rejection corrections]
\label{prop:directly-admissible-slack-rejection-bound}

Under Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound}, suppose that
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \leq e^{(1-\lambda)\tau} \Big[ &e^{n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star} \\&-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q) \Big].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:directly-admissible-slack-rejection-bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&e^{n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star}-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)\\ &\qquad =\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star} \left(1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\right) \right],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:randomised-coupled-alternative-expression}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q) \geq \Gamma_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q).
\label{eq:randomised-slack-dominates-rejection}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Whenever $1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}>0$, one has $S_{n,\lambda}^\star\leq\tau$. Therefore
\begin{equation*}
1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star} \leq e^{(1-\lambda)
(\tau-S_{n,\lambda}^\star)} \left( 1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star} \right).
\end{equation*}
For every $Q\in\mathcal Q$,
\begin{align*}
\mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}
\right]&\leq e^{(1-\lambda)\tau} \mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[ e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star} \left( 1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star} \right) \right].
\end{align*}
Taking the supremum over $Q$ and using the definition of
$\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}$ proves
\eqref{eq:directly-admissible-slack-rejection-bound} and
\eqref{eq:randomised-coupled-alternative-expression}.

The uniform alternative side R\'enyi inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[ e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star} \right] \leq e^{n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star}
\end{equation*}
for every $Q\in\mathcal Q$. Hence the first two terms inside the infimum defining $\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}$ have nonnegative difference, which proves \eqref{eq:randomised-slack-dominates-rejection}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Taking
\begin{equation*}
(\tau,\eta)
=
(\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1)
\end{equation*}
recovers the deterministic bound with slack and rejection corrections.
Taking instead
\begin{equation*}
(\tau,\eta)=(\tau_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}, \eta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}})
\end{equation*}
gives a refined upper bound for the calibrated projected test.

\subsection{Finite blocklength gap decomposition}
\label{subsec:finite-sample-gap-decomposition}

To compare the different refinements at the same order and for the same
projected log--likelihood ratio, define the raw exponential bound
\begin{equation}
B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{exp}}(r):= e^{(1-\lambda)\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}
+n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star},
\label{eq:raw-exponential-projected-bound}
\end{equation}
the bound with slack and rejection corrections at the same threshold
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r):=e^{(1-\lambda)\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}} \Big[&e^{n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star} \\&-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)\Big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:slack-rejection-bound-at-renyi-threshold}
\end{equation}
and the actual worst case Type II error of the deterministic projected test
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}(r):=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n}\left[1-\psi_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda,\star}\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:actual-projected-error-at-renyi-threshold}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite blocklength hierarchy and gap decomposition]
\label{prop:finite-sample-gap-decomposition}

Under the assumptions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound}, and using the
nonnegative measurable-version convention above for
$e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}$, for every $n\geq1$, $r>0$, and
$0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)&\leq\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}} (r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\\&\leq\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}(r) \\&\leq B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r)\\&\leq B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{exp}}(r).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:finite-sample-projected-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{exp}}(r)-\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) ={}& \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{corr}} + \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{weight}} \\ &+ \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}} + \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{proj}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:finite-sample-additive-gap-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
\Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{corr}} &:= B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{exp}}(r) - B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r), \\ \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{weight}} &:= B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r) - \beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}(r), \\ \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}} &:= \beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}(r) - \beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}} (r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q), \\ \Delta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{proj}} &:= \beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) -\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q). 
\end{align*}
Each term is nonnegative.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Every $(\tau,\eta)\in\mathcal C_{n,\lambda}(r)$ induces a test in
$\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$. Comparing the infima over the unrestricted and
restricted feasible families therefore gives
$\beta_n^\star\leq\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} makes the
deterministic projected rule with
$(\tau,\eta)=(\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1)$ a member of
$\mathcal C_{n,\lambda}(r)$. The definition of the restricted infimum in
\eqref{eq:calibrated-projected-value} consequently gives
$\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{cal}}\leq\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}$, without
requiring that the infimum be attained.

For the chosen directly admissible test, the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:directly-admissible-slack-rejection-bound}
establishes the stronger inequality
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}\left[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\right]\\
&\qquad\leq e^{(1-\lambda)\tau}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{Q^n}\left[
e^{(\lambda-1)S_{n,\lambda}^\star}
\left(1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
At $(\tau,\eta)=(\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1)$, the left-hand side is
$\beta_{n,\lambda}^{\min}(r)$, while
\eqref{eq:randomised-coupled-alternative-expression} identifies the
right-hand side with $B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r)$. This proves the
third inequality.

The measurable-version convention makes the integrand defining
$\Gamma_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)$ nonnegative. Hence
\eqref{eq:randomised-slack-dominates-rejection} gives
\begin{equation*}
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)
\geq
\Gamma_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)
\geq0.
\end{equation*}
The definitions of the two bounds therefore give
\begin{equation*}
B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{exp}}(r)-B_{n,\lambda}^{\mathrm{sr}}(r)
=
e^{(1-\lambda)\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min}}
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n,\tau_{\lambda,r}^{\min},1}^{\lambda}(\mathcal Q)
\geq0,
\end{equation*}
which proves the fourth inequality and hence
\eqref{eq:finite-sample-projected-hierarchy}. Thus all four adjacent differences are nonnegative, and summing their definitions cancels the four intermediate quantities and gives exactly \eqref{eq:finite-sample-additive-gap-decomposition}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The four nonnegative terms in \eqref{eq:finite-sample-additive-gap-decomposition} isolate distinct sources of loss. The correction term is the improvement obtained by retaining the slack and rejection contributions in the R\'enyi evaluation. The weighting term is the remaining gap between the corrected expression and the actual Type II error of the deterministic projected test. The calibration term is the loss from using the closed form threshold rather than the calibrated threshold. The projection term is the loss from restricting the minimax problem to threshold tests based on the fixed projected log--likelihood ratio. The constant randomised test is excluded because it is a different decision rule, although any analytical upper bound may still be replaced by
its minimum with $1-e^{-nr}$.

\section{The fixed Type I error regime}
\label{sec:fixed-type-one-error}
The preceding sections consider a worst case Type I error that decays
exponentially. We now treat the distinct boundary regime in which the
Type I error is constrained by a fixed level.

\emph{Assumptions.} Throughout this section,
$\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,d\}$,
$\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\subseteq\Delta_d$ are nonempty, compact, and
convex, every distribution in $\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q$ has full
support, and $0<\varepsilon<1$ is fixed independently of $n$.
Consequently, Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-alphabet-projected-bound} applies
for every $0<\lambda<1$.

Define, locally in this section,
\begin{equation*}
\Phi_{n,\varepsilon}(\mathcal P):=\left\{
\varphi_n\in\mathsf T_n:\alpha_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal P)\leq\varepsilon
\right\},
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q):=\inf_{\varphi_n\in\Phi_{n,\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)}\beta_n(\varphi_n;\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
We also write
\begin{equation*}
D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q):=\min_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P\\Q\in\mathcal Q}}
D(P\|Q)
\end{equation*}
for the forward directed composite KL separation.

\begin{proposition}[Projected fixed level bound]
\label{prop:projected-fixed-level-bound}
For every $n\geq1$ and $0<\lambda<1$, set
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda^\star:=D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\tau_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\min}:=\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)-n(1-\lambda)D_\lambda^\star}{\lambda}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
\psi_{n,\tau_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\min},1}^{\lambda,\star} \in \Phi_{n,\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\leq\min\Bigg\{&1-\varepsilon,\\&\exp\left[-\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}\left(nD_\lambda^\star-\log\frac1\varepsilon
\right)\right]\Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Substituting $\tau_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\min}$ into \eqref{eq:abstract-null-tail} gives
\begin{equation*}
-\lambda\tau_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\min}+n(\lambda-1)D_\lambda^\star = \log\varepsilon.
\end{equation*}
Hence the projected test satisfies the fixed Type I constraint. Substitution
into \eqref{eq:abstract-alternative-tail} gives the exponential term in the
statement. The constant randomised test $\varphi_n\equiv\varepsilon$ has
Type I error $\varepsilon$ and Type II error $1-\varepsilon$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{lemma}[Limit at order zero]
\label{lem:reverse-renyi-order-zero}
Set
\begin{equation*}
\kappa := \min_{\substack{R\in\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q\\x\in\mathcal X}}
R(x)>0, \quad M:=\log\frac1\kappa.
\end{equation*}
Then, for every $P\in\mathcal P$, $Q\in\mathcal Q$, and
$0<\lambda<1$,
\begin{equation*}
0 \leq \log Z_\lambda(Q,P)+\lambda D(P\|Q) \leq \frac{M^2}{2}\lambda^2.
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
D(P\|Q)-\frac{M^2}{2}\lambda \leq \frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}D_\lambda(Q\|P) \leq D(P\|Q),
\end{equation*}
uniformly over the pair class, and
\begin{equation*}
D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)-\frac{M^2}{2}\lambda \leq \frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda} D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P) \leq D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
In particular,
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow 0}
\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}
D_\lambda(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)=D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Let
\begin{equation*}
h_{P,Q}(x):=\log\frac{Q(x)}{P(x)}.
\end{equation*}
Uniform full support gives $|h_{P,Q}|\leq M$, while
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_P[h_{P,Q}]=-D(P\|Q),\quad Z_\lambda(Q,P)=\mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda h_{P,Q}}].
\end{equation*}
Jensen's inequality and Hoeffding's lemma yield
\begin{equation*}
0 \leq \log\mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda h_{P,Q}}] -\lambda\mathbb E_P[h_{P,Q}]\leq\frac{M^2}{2}\lambda^2.
\end{equation*}
Since
\begin{equation*}
\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda}D_\lambda(Q\|P) =-\frac1\lambda\log Z_\lambda(Q,P),
\end{equation*}
the pairwise bounds follow. Taking the minimum over $(P,Q)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q$ gives the composite bounds.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed level composite Chernoff--Stein exponent]
\label{thm:fixed-level-composite-stein}
For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$,
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{n\to\infty}
-\frac1n \log \beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) = D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
If $D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)=0$, then, more precisely,
\begin{equation*}
\beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)= 1-\varepsilon \quad \text{for every }n\geq1.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Fix $P\in\mathcal P$ and $Q\in\mathcal Q$. Every test in
$\Phi_{n,\varepsilon}(\mathcal P)$ is admissible for the simple pair
$(P,Q)$, and its worst case Type II error is at least its Type II error
under $Q^n$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\geq
\beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(\varepsilon;P,Q),
\end{equation*}
where the right side denotes the simple binary value under the fixed Type I
constraint $\varepsilon$. The Chernoff--Stein lemma~\cite{cover2006elements} gives
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{n\to\infty}
-\frac1n \log \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star(\varepsilon;P,Q) = D(P\|Q)
\end{equation*}
Minimising over the pair class therefore yields
\begin{equation*}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
-\frac1n \log \beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\leq D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}

For the reverse inequality, take $\lambda_n=n^{-1/2}$ for $n\geq2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:projected-fixed-level-bound} and Lemma~\ref{lem:reverse-renyi-order-zero} give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
-\frac1n \log \beta_{n,\varepsilon}^\star(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq{}& D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q) - \frac{M^2}{2\sqrt n} \\ &- \frac{1-\lambda_n}{n\lambda_n} \log\frac1\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The last two terms vanish, proving the matching lower limit. If $D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)=0$, compactness and uniform full support imply that the minimum is attained at some common law $R\in\mathcal P\cap\mathcal Q$. Every admissible test then has Type II error at least $1-\varepsilon$ under $R^n$, and the constant test $\varphi_n\equiv\varepsilon$ attains equality.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Change of KL orientation]
\label{rem:fixed-level-kl-orientation}
The fixed level exponent is the forward separation $D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)$, whereas the critical rate under the exponentially decaying Type I constraint is $D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)$. The distinction is operational rather than notational. At fixed level, the relevant projected orders satisfy $\lambda\downarrow0$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:reverse-renyi-order-zero} shows that the reverse R\'enyi family converges, after rescaling, to forward KL. By contrast, the exponential critical rate is obtained from orders tending to one. The same lemma also identifies the zero rate boundary of the composite Hoeffding exponent. Whenever $D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)>0$, compactness and full support also give $D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P)>0$. Hence, choosing $\lambda=\sqrt r$ gives, for all sufficiently small subcritical $r>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q)-\left(\frac{M^2}{2}+1\right)\sqrt r+r \leq{}& E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \\ \leq{}& D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
and hence
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{r\downarrow0}
E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)=D(\mathcal P\|\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
\end{remark}

\section{Exact reduction at finite blocklength}
\label{sec:exact-closure-projected-score-ordering}

Section~\ref{sec:calibrated-projected-tests} introduces a restricted minimax problem over randomised threshold tests based on a fixed projected log--likelihood ratio. Its calibrated value is an upper bound on the unrestricted minimax Type II error, but equality need not hold. Likewise, the R\'enyi--Hoeffding saddle point in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle} identifies the exact subcritical Type II error exponent without implying exact reduction at finite blocklength.

We now give sufficient ordering conditions under which the selected pair attains the worst case Type I and Type II errors of the corresponding threshold test. Exact minimax equality follows when this test is also NP optimal for the selected pair.

We continue to use the randomised projected threshold test defined in \eqref{eq:randomised-projected-threshold-test}. The projected log--likelihood ratio is said to be composite ordered at $(P_\lambda^\star,Q_\lambda^\star)$ if, for every $t\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{align}
P\{h_\lambda^\star\geq t\}
&\leq P_\lambda^\star\{h_\lambda^\star\geq t\},
\quad \forall P\in\mathcal P,
\label{eq:null-projected-score-ordering}
\\ Q\{h_\lambda^\star\leq t\}
&\leq Q_\lambda^\star\{h_\lambda^\star\leq t\},
\quad \forall Q\in\mathcal Q.
\label{eq:alternative-projected-score-ordering}
\end{align}
Since the logarithm is increasing, these conditions are equivalent to the single sample minimax robustness inequalities for the selected pair in \cite[Definition~II.2]{gul2026structural}.


\begin{theorem}[Worst case errors under projected ordering]
\label{thm:projected-score-ordering-exact-closure}
Assume that \eqref{eq:null-projected-score-ordering} and \eqref{eq:alternative-projected-score-ordering} hold. Then, for every $n\geq1$, $\tau\in\mathbb R$, and $0\leq\eta\leq1$,
\begin{align}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P} \mathbb E_{P^n} \bigl[\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr] &= \mathbb E_{(P_\lambda^\star)^n} \bigl[\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr],
\label{eq:projected-score-null-exact-closure}\\
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \mathbb E_{Q^n} \bigl[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr] &= \mathbb E_{(Q_\lambda^\star)^n} \bigl[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr].
\label{eq:projected-score-alternative-exact-closure}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} implies that $\mathcal M_{n,\lambda}^\star$ is null under every product law generated by $\mathcal P$ or $\mathcal Q$. Therefore,
\begin{equation*}
S_{n,\lambda}^\star = \sum_{i=1}^n h_\lambda^\star(X_i)
\end{equation*}
almost surely under all relevant product laws.

The null side ordering and preservation of stochastic order under independent sums give
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{i=1}^n h_\lambda^\star(X_i) \ \text{under }P^n \ \leq_{\mathrm{st}}\ \sum_{i=1}^n h_\lambda^\star(X_i) \ \text{under }(P_\lambda^\star)^n
\end{equation*}
for every $P\in\mathcal P$. Since
\begin{equation*}
s\longmapsto \mathbf 1\{s>\tau\} + \eta\mathbf 1\{s=\tau\}
\end{equation*}
is nondecreasing,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{P^n} \bigl[\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr] \leq \mathbb E_{(P_\lambda^\star)^n} \bigl[\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr].
\end{equation*}
Because $P_\lambda^\star\in\mathcal P$, equality holds after taking the supremum over $P$.

The alternative side ordering gives the reverse stochastic order under $Q^n$ and $(Q_\lambda^\star)^n$. Since
\begin{equation*}
s\longmapsto 1-\mathbf 1\{s>\tau\}-\eta\mathbf 1\{s=\tau\}
\end{equation*}
is nonincreasing,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{Q^n} \bigl[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr] \leq \mathbb E_{(Q_\lambda^\star)^n} \bigl[1-\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr].
\end{equation*}
Because $Q_\lambda^\star\in\mathcal Q$, equality again holds after taking the supremum.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact reduction for the projected pair]
\label{cor:exact-reduction-projected-simple-pair}

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:projected-score-ordering-exact-closure}. Suppose that, for the given $n$ and $r$, the test $\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}$ is optimal for testing $(P_\lambda^\star)^n$ against $(Q_\lambda^\star)^n$ under the constraint
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{(P_\lambda^\star)^n} \bigl[\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}\bigr] \leq e^{-nr}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)=\beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P_\lambda^\star,Q_\lambda^\star).
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Every test in $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ is admissible for the selected simple pair, and $Q_\lambda^\star\in\mathcal Q$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star
(r;P_\lambda^\star,Q_\lambda^\star).
\end{equation*}

Conversely, the null side identity in \eqref{eq:projected-score-null-exact-closure} makes $\psi_{n,\tau,\eta}^{\lambda,\star}$ admissible for the composite problem. The alternative side identity in \eqref{eq:projected-score-alternative-exact-closure}, together with its assumed optimality for the selected pair, gives the reverse inequality.
\end{IEEEproof}


\subsection{Ordered one-parameter families}
\label{subsec:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}

Consider a natural exponential family on
$(\mathcal X,\mathcal F,\mu)$, where $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite. Assume that
the family has common support and densities
\begin{equation}
p_\theta(x)
=
h_0(x)
\exp\{\theta T(x)-\psi(\theta)\},
\qquad
\theta\in\Theta,
\label{eq:one-parameter-nef}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta\subseteq\mathbb R$ is open, $h_0>0$ on the common support,
and $\psi$ is finite, differentiable, and strictly convex. For
$\theta_1<\theta_2$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{p_{\theta_2}(x)}{p_{\theta_1}(x)}
=
\exp\Bigl\{
(\theta_2-\theta_1)T(x)
-
\bigl[\psi(\theta_2)-\psi(\theta_1)\bigr]
\Bigr\},
\label{eq:nef-monotone-likelihood-ratio}
\end{equation}
so the family has monotone likelihood ratio in $T$.

Let the null and alternative parameter sets be compact intervals contained
in $\Theta$, and suppose that
\begin{equation*}
\theta_-^{\mathcal P}
<
\theta_+^{\mathcal P}
<
\theta_-^{\mathcal Q}
<
\theta_+^{\mathcal Q}.
\end{equation*}
Define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal P
&:=
\left\{
P_\theta:
\theta\in
[\theta_-^{\mathcal P},\theta_+^{\mathcal P}]
\right\},
\\
\mathcal Q
&:=
\left\{
P_\theta:
\theta\in
[\theta_-^{\mathcal Q},\theta_+^{\mathcal Q}]
\right\}.
\end{align*}
The adjacent endpoint pair is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm e}
:=
P_{\theta_+^{\mathcal P}},
\qquad
Q_{\mathrm e}
:=
P_{\theta_-^{\mathcal Q}}.
\label{eq:ordered-adjacent-endpoint-pair}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact endpoint reduction]
\label{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}

Under \eqref{eq:one-parameter-nef}--\eqref{eq:ordered-adjacent-endpoint-pair}, for every $n\geq1$ and $r>0$,
\begin{equation}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) = \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star
(r;P_{\mathrm e},Q_{\mathrm e}).
\label{eq:ordered-composite-endpoint-reduction}
\end{equation}
If the alternative interval lies strictly to the left of the null interval, the same conclusion holds with the opposite adjacent endpoints and a randomised lower tail test.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
By the NP lemma and \eqref{eq:nef-monotone-likelihood-ratio}, an optimal test for $(P_{\mathrm e})^n$ against $(Q_{\mathrm e})^n$ at Type I level $e^{-nr}$ may be written as
\begin{equation*}
\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}=\mathbf 1\{T_n>t\}+\eta\mathbf 1\{T_n=t\}, \ 0\leq\eta\leq1,
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
T_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n T(X_i).
\end{equation*}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:nef-stochastic-monotonicity}, the distribution of $T_n$ is stochastically increasing in $\theta$. Since $\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}$ is nondecreasing in $T_n$, for every $P_\theta\in\mathcal P$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{P_\theta^n} [\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}] \leq \mathbb E_{(P_{\mathrm e})^n} [\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}] \leq e^{-nr}.
\end{equation*}
Thus
\begin{equation*}
\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}

The miss function $1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}$ is nonincreasing in $T_n$. The same stochastic ordering therefore gives, for every $Q=P_\theta\in\mathcal Q$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{Q^n} [1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}] \leq \mathbb E_{(Q_{\mathrm e})^n}
[1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}].
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) &\leq \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{Q^n} [1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}]\\ &= \mathbb E_{(Q_{\mathrm e})^n} [1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm e}]\\ &= \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P_{\mathrm e},Q_{\mathrm e}).
\end{align*}

Conversely, every test in $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ satisfies the Type I constraint under $P_{\mathrm e}$, and $Q_{\mathrm e}\in\mathcal Q$. Therefore
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star
(r;P_{\mathrm e},Q_{\mathrm e}).
\end{equation*}
Combining the two inequalities proves \eqref{eq:ordered-composite-endpoint-reduction}.

If the alternative interval lies to the left of the null interval, the same argument applies to the statistic $-T$ and gives the corresponding randomised lower-tail test.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite blocklength bounds for the endpoint pair]
\label{cor:ordered-endpoint-renyi-bounds}

Under Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}, for every $0<\lambda<1$ for which the endpoint divergence is finite,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) & \leq \min\Bigg\{ 1-e^{-nr},\\ & \exp\left[ -n\frac{1-\lambda}{\lambda} \bigl\{ D_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e}\|P_{\mathrm e})-r \bigr\} \right] \Bigg\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
For every $\lambda>1$ for which the endpoint divergence is finite,
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\geq 1-\exp\left\{-n\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda} \bigl[r-D_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e}\|P_{\mathrm e})\bigr]_+ \right\}.
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-renyi-achievability} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-sample-composite-renyi-converse} to the singleton classes $\{P_{\mathrm e}\}$ and $\{Q_{\mathrm e}\}$. Then use \eqref{eq:ordered-composite-endpoint-reduction}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The adjacent endpoint pair is also consistent with the directed R\'enyi projection. For every order $\lambda>0$, $\lambda\neq1$, for which
\begin{equation*}
\lambda\theta_{\mathcal Q} + (1-\lambda)\theta_{\mathcal P} \in\Theta
\end{equation*}
for all relevant parameter pairs, the natural exponential family formula gives
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\mathcal P}} & D_\lambda (P_{\theta_{\mathcal Q}}\|P_{\theta_{\mathcal P}}) = \psi'(\theta_{\mathcal P}) - \psi'\bigl( \lambda\theta_{\mathcal Q} + (1-\lambda)\theta_{\mathcal P} \bigr)<0, \\ & \frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{\mathcal Q}} D_\lambda (P_{\theta_{\mathcal Q}}\|P_{\theta_{\mathcal P}}) \\ &= \frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1} \Bigl[ \psi'\bigl( \lambda\theta_{\mathcal Q} + (1-\lambda)\theta_{\mathcal P} \bigr) - \psi'(\theta_{\mathcal Q}) \Bigr] >0.
\end{align*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e}\|P_{\mathrm e}) = \inf_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \inf_{P\in\mathcal P} D_\lambda(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}

These exact reductions rely on stochastic ordering of the likelihood ratio. They are additional to the projected achievability result and do not follow from R\'enyi projection alone.

\section{Examples of ordered exact reduction}
\label{sec:ordered-exact-closure-examples}

The continuous mixture example in Subsection~\ref{subsec:continuous-convex-gaussian-mixtures} illustrates the convex dominated projection theorem. The examples below instead illustrate exact reduction at finite blocklength under monotone likelihood ratio ordering. The Bernoulli family is also covered by the finite alphabet projection result. The Poisson, Gaussian, and exponential families are generally not convex as density classes and are covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}. In each case, the adjacent endpoint pair is least favourable for the randomised likelihood ratio test.

%##################################################
\subsection{Bernoulli family}

Let $P_\theta=\operatorname{Bern}(p)$, where
\begin{equation*}
\theta=\log\frac{p}{1-p},\quad p= \frac{e^\theta}{1+e^\theta}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
p_\theta(x)=\exp\{x\theta-\psi(\theta)\},\quad\psi(\theta)=\log(1+e^\theta),
\end{equation*}
for $x\in\{0,1\}$. Since $\theta$ is strictly increasing in $p$, separated intervals
\begin{equation*}
0<p_-^{\mathcal P}<p_+^{\mathcal P}<p_-^{\mathcal Q}<p_+^{\mathcal Q}<1
\end{equation*}
satisfy the ordered reduction condition. The adjacent endpoint pair is
\begin{equation*}
P_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{Bern}(p_+^{\mathcal P}),\quad Q_{\mathrm e}= \operatorname{Bern}(p_-^{\mathcal Q}).
\end{equation*}
The vector $(1-p,p)$ is affine in $p$, so these intervals are compact convex density classes on a finite alphabet. All positive R\'enyi orders are finite. Because the sufficient statistic is discrete, exact attainment of the prescribed Type I level may require randomisation at the threshold.
%##################################################
\subsection{Poisson family}
Let $P_\theta=\operatorname{Poisson}(\eta)$, with
\begin{equation*}
\theta=\log\eta, \quad \eta=e^\theta.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
p_\theta(x)=\frac{1}{x!} \exp\{x\theta-e^\theta\},\quad x\in\mathbb N_0.
\end{equation*}
Since $\theta$ is increasing in the mean $\eta$, separated mean intervals
\begin{equation*}
\eta_-^{\mathcal P}< \eta_+^{\mathcal P} <\eta_-^{\mathcal Q} < \eta_+^{\mathcal Q}
\end{equation*}
have adjacent endpoint pair
\begin{equation*}
P_{\mathrm e} = \operatorname{Poisson}(\eta_+^{\mathcal P}), \quad Q_{\mathrm e} = \operatorname{Poisson}(\eta_-^{\mathcal Q}).
\end{equation*}
A mixture of two distinct Poisson laws is generally not Poisson, so this
family does not fall under the convex projection theorem. Exact endpoint
reduction instead follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}. For endpoint means $\eta_P,\eta_Q$,
\begin{equation*}
Z_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e},P_{\mathrm e}) =\exp\{-\lambda\eta_Q-(1-\lambda)\eta_P +\eta_Q^\lambda\eta_P^{1-\lambda}\}.
\end{equation*}
The sufficient statistic is integer valued, so exact attainment of the prescribed Type I level may likewise require randomisation at the threshold.
%##################################################
\subsection{Gaussian location family with known variance}

Let $P_\theta=N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, where $\sigma^2>0$ is fixed. Taking
\begin{equation*}
\theta = \frac{\mu}{\sigma^2},
\end{equation*}
the density can be written as
\begin{equation*}
p_\theta(x) = h(x)\exp\{\theta x-\psi(\theta)\},
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
h(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}} \exp\left\{-\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2}\right\}, \quad \psi(\theta) =
\frac{\sigma^2\theta^2}{2}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\theta$ is increasing in $\mu$, separated mean intervals
\begin{equation*}
\mu_-^{\mathcal P} < \mu_+^{\mathcal P} < \mu_-^{\mathcal Q}<\mu_+^{\mathcal Q}
\end{equation*}
have adjacent endpoint pair
\begin{equation*}
P_{\mathrm e} = N(\mu_+^{\mathcal P},\sigma^2), \quad Q_{\mathrm e} = N(\mu_-^{\mathcal Q},\sigma^2).
\end{equation*}
The Gaussian location family with fixed variance is not convex as a density class. Exact reduction therefore follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}, rather than from the convex projection result. For every positive order,
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e}\|P_{\mathrm e}) =\lambda\frac{(\mu_Q-\mu_P)^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\end{equation*}
The sample mean boundary is nonatomic, so boundary randomisation is normally immaterial, although the theorem permits it.

%##################################################
\subsection{Exponential family}

Let $X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\rho)$, with rate $\rho>0$, so that
\begin{equation*}
p_\rho(x)=\rho e^{-\rho x},\quad x\ge0.
\end{equation*}
This can be written as a natural exponential family with 
\begin{equation*}
\theta=-\rho<0, \quad T(x)=x, \quad \psi(\theta)=-\log(-\theta).
\end{equation*}
Increasing $\theta$ corresponds to decreasing the rate and increasing the mean, so natural parameter order reverses rate order. If the alternative interval lies to the right of the null interval in natural parameter order, then all null rates exceed all alternative rates. Writing $\rho_P$ and $\rho_Q$ for the nearest endpoints, the selected pair is
\begin{equation*}
P_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{Exp}(\rho_P), \quad Q_{\mathrm e}=\operatorname{Exp}(\rho_Q), \quad \rho_P>\rho_Q.
\end{equation*}
This family is likewise not convex as a density class, and exact reduction follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure}. Define
\begin{equation*}
a_\lambda:=\lambda\rho_Q+(1-\lambda)\rho_P.
\end{equation*}
Its endpoint Hellinger integral is
\begin{equation*}
Z_\lambda(Q_{\mathrm e},P_{\mathrm e})
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\rho_Q^\lambda\rho_P^{1-\lambda}}{a_\lambda},
& a_\lambda>0,\\[1ex]
+\infty,
& a_\lambda\leq0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
For $0<\lambda<1$, the denominator $a_\lambda$ is automatically positive because it is a convex combination of the positive rates $\rho_Q$ and $\rho_P$. For a R\'enyi order $\lambda>1$, the finite-divergence condition required by the reverse converse is
\begin{equation*}
a_\lambda>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda<\frac{\rho_P}{\rho_P-\rho_Q}.
\end{equation*}
No converse optimisation may include orders outside this domain.

\subsection{A continuous convex projection example}
\label{subsec:continuous-convex-gaussian-mixtures}

We illustrate the dominated projection theorem using two convex line
segments of Gaussian mixtures on a nonatomic sample space. These classes are
not Gaussian location families. Therefore,
Theorem~\ref{thm:ordered-one-parameter-exact-composite-closure} does not
apply.

Let
\begin{equation*}
\phi_m(x)
:=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\left\{
-\frac{(x-m)^2}{2}
\right\},
\qquad
x\in\mathbb R,
\end{equation*}
and define
\begin{align*}
p_t(x)
&:=
(1-t)\phi_{-2}(x)+t\phi_1(x),
\\
q_s(x)
&:=
(1-s)\phi_{-3}(x)+s\phi_{1/2}(x),
\end{align*}
where $t,s\in[0,1]$. Let
\begin{align*}
\mathscr P_{\rm mix}
&:=
\{p_t:t\in[0,1]\},
\\
\mathscr Q_{\rm mix}
&:=
\{q_s:s\in[0,1]\},
\end{align*}
and let $\mathcal P_{\rm mix}$ and $\mathcal Q_{\rm mix}$ denote the
corresponding law classes.

Both density classes are convex and compact in $L^1(\mathbb R)$ because
\begin{align*}
\|p_t-p_u\|_1
&=
|t-u|\,\|\phi_1-\phi_{-2}\|_1,
\\
\|q_s-q_v\|_1
&=
|s-v|\,\|\phi_{1/2}-\phi_{-3}\|_1.
\end{align*}
They are therefore weakly compact. Every density in both classes is
strictly positive on $\mathbb R$, so the sum of any selected pair dominates
every law in the two classes. Moreover, for each $0<\lambda<1$,
$Z_\lambda(q_s,p_t)$ is positive and continuous on the compact set
$[0,1]^2$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\inf_{(t,s)\in[0,1]^2}
Z_\lambda(q_s,p_t)
>
0.
\end{equation*}
Thus every joint maximiser has positive Hellinger integral and satisfies
\eqref{eq:selected-pair-domination}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:dominated-projection-uniform-renyi-inequalities}
therefore yields a joint projected pair and the two uniform R\'enyi
inequalities.

For $\lambda=\tfrac12$, the projected pair maximises the Hellinger affinity:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
(t_{1/2}^\star,s_{1/2}^\star)
\in
\operatorname*{argmax}_{(t,s)\in[0,1]^2}
\int_{\mathbb R}
\sqrt{q_s(x)p_t(x)}\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Adaptive quadrature and bounded optimisation give
\begin{align*}
t_{1/2}^\star
&\approx
0.835952,
&
s_{1/2}^\star
&\approx
0.912784,
\\
Z_{1/2}^\star
&\approx
0.977783,
&
D_{1/2}^\star
&=
-2\log Z_{1/2}^\star
\approx
0.0449355.
\end{align*}
Since both weights lie in $(0,1)$, the projected laws are genuine mixtures
rather than endpoints. The corresponding projected log--likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation*}
h_{1/2}^\star(x)
=
\log
\frac{
(1-s_{1/2}^\star)\phi_{-3}(x)
+
s_{1/2}^\star\phi_{1/2}(x)
}{
(1-t_{1/2}^\star)\phi_{-2}(x)
+
t_{1/2}^\star\phi_1(x)
}.
\end{equation*}
Corollary~\ref{cor:dominated-finite-sample-projected-bound} then gives,
for every $n\geq1$ and $r>0$,
\begin{align*}
\beta_n^\star
(r;&\mathcal P_{\rm mix},\mathcal Q_{\rm mix})
\\
&\leq
\min\left\{
1-e^{-nr},
\exp\left[
-n\left(D_{1/2}^\star-r\right)
\right]
\right\}.
\end{align*}
Hence the projected test has exponentially decreasing worst case Type II
error whenever
\begin{equation*}
r
<
D_{1/2}^\star
\approx
0.0449355.
\end{equation*}
This gives a direct continuous application of the dominated projection
theorem. It does not imply that the projected pair is operationally least
favourable or that exact reduction holds at finite blocklength.

\section{Complete one-sided directional derivative}
\label{app:directional-derivative}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Decompose $\mathcal Y$ into
\begin{align*}
S&:=\{y>0\}, & A&:=\{y=0,w>0\}, \\ N&:=\{y=0,w=0\}. &&
\end{align*}
For $0<t\leq1$,
\begin{equation*}
F(y_t) = \int_S w\bigl((1-t)y+tx\bigr)^\alpha\,d\mu + t^\alpha\int_A wx^\alpha\,d\mu.
\end{equation*}
Writing
\begin{equation*}
c:=\int_A wx^\alpha\,d\mu\in[0,\infty],
\end{equation*}
the inequality $y_t\geq(1-t)y$ on $S$ and the assumption $F(y_t)\leq F(y)$ give
\begin{equation*}
(1-t)^\alpha F(y)+t^\alpha c \leq F(y_t) \leq F(y).
\end{equation*}
The preceding inequality implies that $c<\infty$. Since $(1-t)^\alpha\geq1-t$,
\begin{equation*}
0 \leq c \leq F(y)\frac{1-(1-t)^\alpha}{t^\alpha} \leq F(y)t^{1-\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
Letting $t\downarrow0$ yields $c=0$. Since $w>0$ on $A$, it follows that $x=0$ almost everywhere on $A$, which proves
\eqref{eq:directional-no-new-support}. The set $N$ contributes nothing. Define the probability measure
\begin{equation*}
\nu(E):=\frac{1}{F(y)} \int_{E\cap S} wy^\alpha\,d\mu,
\end{equation*}
and set $R=x/y$ on $S$ and $R=0$ on $S^c$. Since $x$ and $y$ are finite almost everywhere and $y>0$ on $S$, the function $R$ is finite and measurable $\nu$ almost surely. The support conclusion gives
\begin{equation*}
\frac{F(y_t)}{F(y)}=\mathbb E_\nu\!\left[(1-t+tR)^\alpha\right].
\end{equation*}
For $0<t\leq1$ and $r\geq0$, define
\begin{equation*}
g_t(r):=\frac{(1-t+tr)^\alpha-1}{t}.
\end{equation*}
The preceding identity and the assumption $F(y_t)\leq F(y)$ imply that $g_t(R)$ is $\nu$ integrable and
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_\nu[g_t(R)]=\frac{F(y_t)-F(y)}{tF(y)} \leq 0.
\end{equation*}
For every $0<t\leq1$, the function $g_t$ is increasing and satisfies
\begin{equation}
g_t(r) \geq g_t(0) = \frac{(1-t)^\alpha-1}{t} \geq -1, \qquad r\geq0.
\label{eq:directional-secant-lower}
\end{equation}
Let $t_k\downarrow0$. Since $g_{t_k}(R)+1$ is nonnegative, Fatou's lemma gives
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_\nu \left[ \liminf_{k\to\infty} \bigl(g_{t_k}(R)+1\bigr) \right] \leq \liminf_{k\to\infty} \mathbb E_\nu \left[ g_{t_k}(R)+1 \right] \leq 1.
\end{equation*}
For every $r\geq0$,
\begin{equation*}
g_t(r) \longrightarrow \alpha(r-1) \quad \text{as }t\downarrow0.
\end{equation*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation*}
1-\alpha+\alpha\mathbb E_\nu[R] \leq 1,
\end{equation*}
and hence
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_\nu[R]\leq1.
\end{equation*}
The bound $\mathbb E_\nu[R]\leq1$ implies that $R\in L^1(\nu)$. Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
\int_S wy^{\alpha-1}x\,d\mu = F(y)\mathbb E_\nu[R] \leq F(y),
\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{eq:directional-moment}.

By concavity,
\begin{equation*}
u^\alpha \leq 1+\alpha(u-1), \quad u\geq0.
\end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*}
g_t(r) \leq \alpha(r-1) \leq \alpha r.
\end{equation*}
Together with \eqref{eq:directional-secant-lower}, this gives 
\begin{equation*}
|g_t(R)| \leq 1+\alpha R.
\end{equation*}
The right side is integrable under $\nu$. Dominated convergence therefore yields
\begin{equation*}
\lim_{t\downarrow0} \mathbb E_\nu[g_t(R)] = \alpha\bigl(\mathbb E_\nu[R]-1\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Multiplying by $F(y)$ proves \eqref{eq:directional-derivative}. Its nonpositive sign follows from
$\mathbb E_\nu[R]\leq1$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{R\'enyi-to-KL minimum convergence}
\label{app:renyi-kl-minimum}
\begin{IEEEproof}
For every fixed pair $(P,Q)$,
\begin{equation*}
D_\lambda(Q\|P) \uparrow D(Q\|P) \quad \text{as }\lambda\uparrow 1
\end{equation*}
in the extended real sense. For $0<\lambda<1$, $D_\lambda$ is weakly lower semicontinuous because $Z_\lambda$ is weakly upper semicontinuous and
\begin{equation*}
z\longmapsto -\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\log z
\end{equation*}
is decreasing, with value $+\infty$ at $z=0$. Let $\lambda_k\uparrow1$, and choose minimisers
\begin{equation*}
(Q_k,P_k) \in \operatorname*{argmin}_{\substack{Q\in\mathcal Q\\P\in\mathcal P}} D_{\lambda_k}(Q\|P).
\end{equation*}
By weak compactness, a subsequence converges to some $(Q^\star,P^\star)\in\mathcal Q\times\mathcal P$. For every fixed $m$ and all sufficiently large $k$, monotonicity in the R\'enyi order gives
\begin{equation*}
d_{\lambda_k} = D_{\lambda_k}(Q_k\|P_k) \geq D_{\lambda_m}(Q_k\|P_k).
\end{equation*}
Weak lower semicontinuity therefore yields
\begin{equation*}
\liminf_{k\to\infty} d_{\lambda_k} \geq D_{\lambda_m}(Q^\star\|P^\star).
\end{equation*}
Letting $m\to\infty$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\liminf_{k\to\infty} d_{\lambda_k} \geq D(Q^\star\|P^\star) \geq d_1.
\end{equation*}
Conversely, $D_\lambda(Q\|P)\leq D(Q\|P)$ for every pair, so $d_\lambda\leq d_1$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
d_\lambda\uparrow d_1 \qquad
\text{as }\lambda\uparrow1.
\end{equation*}
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Polynomial order at the Hoeffding saddle}
\label{app:composite-polynomial-order}

This appendix proves the polynomial refinement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle} using a uniform
consequence of the Berry--Esseen inequality. The argument does not require
separate treatments of the lattice and nonlattice cases.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform weighted residual bounds on a finite alphabet]
\label{lem:uniform-finite-range-weighted-residuals}

Let $\mathcal X$ be finite, let $g:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R$ be
nonconstant, and let
$\mathfrak W\subseteq\Delta_{\lvert\mathcal X\rvert}$ be compact. Assume
that every $W\in\mathfrak W$ has full support. For
$X_1,\ldots,X_n$ i.i.d.\ under $W$, define
\begin{equation*}
G_n
:=
\sum_{i=1}^n g(X_i),
\qquad
m_W
:=
\mathbb E_W[g].
\end{equation*}
Set
\begin{equation*}
\Delta_{n,W}
:=
G_n-nm_W.
\end{equation*}

For each fixed $L>0$, there exists $A_L<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\substack{W\in\mathfrak W\\y\in\mathbb R}}
W^n\{y\leq G_n<y+L\}
\leq
\frac{A_L}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
n\geq1.
\label{eq:uniform-finite-range-concentration}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $0<s_0<s_1<\infty$, there exists $A<\infty$
such that, for every $W\in\mathfrak W$, $s_0\leq s\leq s_1$, and
$t\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb E_{W^n}\!\left[
e^{-s(G_n-t)}
\mathbf 1\{G_n\geq t\}
\right]
&\leq
\frac{A}{\sqrt n},
\label{eq:uniform-right-weighted-residual}
\\
\mathbb E_{W^n}\!\left[
e^{s(G_n-t)}
\mathbf 1\{G_n<t\}
\right]
&\leq
\frac{A}{\sqrt n}.
\label{eq:uniform-left-weighted-residual}
\end{align}

Moreover, for each fixed $0\leq M<\infty$, there exist
$0<a_M<A_M<\infty$ and $n_M<\infty$ such that, whenever
$W\in\mathfrak W$, $s_0\leq s\leq s_1$, $n\geq n_M$, and
$\lvert u\rvert\leq M\log n$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_M\frac{e^{-su}}{\sqrt n}
&\leq
\mathbb E_{W^n}\!\left[
e^{-s\Delta_{n,W}}
\mathbf 1\{\Delta_{n,W}\geq u\}
\right]
\\
&\leq
A_M\frac{e^{-su}}{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uniform-right-residual-two-sided}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_M\frac{e^{su}}{\sqrt n}
&\leq
\mathbb E_{W^n}\!\left[
e^{s\Delta_{n,W}}
\mathbf 1\{\Delta_{n,W}<u\}
\right]
\\
&\leq
A_M\frac{e^{su}}{\sqrt n}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uniform-left-residual-two-sided}
\end{equation}
The same conclusions hold, after changing the constants, if a strict
endpoint is replaced by a weak endpoint or conversely.
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Since $\mathcal X$ is finite and $\mathfrak W$ is compact with full
support, there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that
\begin{equation*}
W(x)\geq\varepsilon
\qquad
\text{for every }W\in\mathfrak W
\text{ and }x\in\mathcal X.
\end{equation*}
Because $g$ is nonconstant, continuity of
$W\mapsto\operatorname{Var}_W(g)$ gives constants
$0<v_0\leq v_1<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation*}
v_0
\leq
\operatorname{Var}_W(g)
\leq
v_1
\qquad
\text{for every }W\in\mathfrak W.
\end{equation*}
The centred third absolute moment is also uniformly bounded over
$\mathfrak W$. Hence the Berry--Esseen inequality
\cite[Th.~44, p.~2329]{polyanskiy2010channel} gives a constant
$B<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\substack{W\in\mathfrak W\\z\in\mathbb R}}
\left|
W^n\!\left\{
\frac{G_n-nm_W}
{\sqrt{n\operatorname{Var}_W(g)}}
\leq z
\right\}
-\Phi(z)
\right|
\leq
\frac{B}{\sqrt n}.
\label{eq:uniform-berry-esseen-polynomial-order}
\end{equation}

An interval of length $L$ on the original scale has standardised length at
most
\begin{equation*}
\frac{L}{\sqrt{nv_0}}.
\end{equation*}
Its Gaussian probability is therefore at most
\begin{equation*}
\frac{L}{\sqrt{2\pi nv_0}}.
\end{equation*}
Applying
\eqref{eq:uniform-berry-esseen-polynomial-order} at both endpoints proves
\eqref{eq:uniform-finite-range-concentration}. The same estimate applies
to point masses because every singleton is contained in an interval of
fixed positive length.

To prove \eqref{eq:uniform-right-weighted-residual}, partition
$[t,\infty)$ into
\begin{equation*}
I_j
:=
[t+jL,t+(j+1)L),
\qquad
j\geq0.
\end{equation*}
On $I_j$,
\begin{equation*}
e^{-s(G_n-t)}
\leq
e^{-s_0jL}.
\end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{W^n}\!\left[
e^{-s(G_n-t)}
\mathbf 1\{G_n\geq t\}
\right]
\leq
\frac{A_L}{\sqrt n}
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
e^{-s_0jL},
\end{equation*}
which proves \eqref{eq:uniform-right-weighted-residual}. Partitioning
$(-\infty,t)$ in the same way from right to left proves
\eqref{eq:uniform-left-weighted-residual}.

It remains to prove the lower bounds. Uniformly over
$W\in\mathfrak W$ and $\lvert u\rvert\leq M\log n$, the Gaussian
probability of
\begin{equation*}
u+H
\leq
G_n-nm_W
<
u+2H
\end{equation*}
is
\begin{equation*}
\frac{H}
{\sqrt{2\pi n\operatorname{Var}_W(g)}}
\bigl[1+o(1)\bigr].
\end{equation*}
The remainder is uniform because
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\lvert u\rvert\leq M\log n}
\frac{\lvert u\rvert}{\sqrt n}
\longrightarrow0
\end{equation*}
and the variances lie in $[v_0,v_1]$. Choose $H>0$ so that
\begin{equation*}
\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\pi v_1}}
>
2B+2.
\end{equation*}
For all sufficiently large $n$, the Gaussian interval probability is at
least $(2B+1)/\sqrt n$. Applying the Berry--Esseen bound at both endpoints
therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
W^n\!\left\{
u+H
\leq
G_n-nm_W
<
u+2H
\right\}
\geq
\frac{1}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation*}
On this interval,
\begin{equation*}
e^{-s(G_n-nm_W)}
\geq
e^{-su}e^{-2s_1H}.
\end{equation*}
This proves the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:uniform-right-residual-two-sided}.

The same argument applied to
\begin{equation*}
u-2H
<
G_n-nm_W
\leq
u-H
\end{equation*}
gives an interval contained in the left tail with probability at least
$n^{-1/2}$. On this interval,
\begin{equation*}
e^{s(G_n-nm_W)}
\geq
e^{su}e^{-2s_1H}.
\end{equation*}
This proves the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:uniform-left-residual-two-sided}.

The upper bounds in
\eqref{eq:uniform-right-residual-two-sided} and
\eqref{eq:uniform-left-residual-two-sided} follow from
\eqref{eq:uniform-right-weighted-residual} and
\eqref{eq:uniform-left-weighted-residual} with $t=nm_W+u$, after
factoring out $e^{-su}$ and $e^{su}$, respectively. The point mass estimate
from \eqref{eq:uniform-finite-range-concentration} shows that the same
bounds hold for either strict or weak endpoints after adjusting the
constants.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{theorem}[Composite polynomial order at the Hoeffding saddle]
\label{thm:composite-polynomial-order} 
Let $\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,d\}$, and let $\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\subseteq\Delta_d$ be nonempty, compact, and convex. Assume that every distribution in $\mathcal P\cup\mathcal Q$ has full support, and fix
\begin{equation*}
0<r<D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
Let $(\lambda_r^\star,P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)$ be any saddle triple from Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle}, and write
\begin{equation*}
\lambda:=\lambda_r^\star, \quad b:=1-\lambda, \quad E:=E_{\rm H}(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q).
\end{equation*}
Define
\begin{equation*}
h(x) := \log\frac{Q_r^\star(x)}{P_r^\star(x)}, \quad \mu:=r-E, \quad S_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n h(X_i).
\end{equation*}

Then there exist constants $0<c<C<\infty$ and $n_0<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
c\,n^{-1/(2\lambda)}e^{-nE} &\leq \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \\&\leq C\,n^{-1/(2\lambda)}e^{-nE},\quad n\geq n_0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:composite-polynomial-order}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
-\frac{1}{n} \log \beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) = E + \frac{\log n}{2\lambda n} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).
\label{eq:composite-polynomial-logarithmic-refinement}
\end{equation}
More precisely, there exists a constant $B<\infty$ such that the projected log--likelihood ratio test
\begin{equation}
\varphi_n^{(B)} := \mathbf 1\!\left\{ S_n \geq n\mu - \frac{\log n}{2\lambda} + B \right\}
\label{eq:polynomial-order-projected-test}
\end{equation}
belongs to $\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ for every $n\geq n_0$ and satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \mathbb E_{Q^n}
\left[ 1-\varphi_n^{(B)} \right] \leq C\,n^{-1/(2\lambda)}e^{-nE}.
\label{eq:polynomial-order-projected-upper}
\end{equation}
For the selected simple binary problem,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P_r^\star,Q_r^\star) = \Theta\!\left( n^{-1/(2\lambda)}e^{-nE} \right).
\label{eq:selected-pair-polynomial-order}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the threshold of an exact randomised NP test has the form
\begin{equation}
n\mu - \frac{\log n}{2\lambda} + O(1).
\label{eq:selected-pair-polynomial-threshold}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For brevity, write
\begin{equation*}
P^\star:=P_r^\star, \quad Q^\star:=Q_r^\star,
\end{equation*}
and define
\begin{equation*}
Z:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X} Q^\star(x)^\lambda P^\star(x)^b.
\end{equation*}
The tilted distribution in \eqref{eq:hoeffding-saddle-tilted-law} is
\begin{equation*}
R(x) := \frac{Q^\star(x)^\lambda P^\star(x)^b}{Z}.
\end{equation*}
Let
\begin{equation*}
m:=\mathbb E_R[h].
\end{equation*}
Direct calculation gives
\begin{align*}
D(R\|P^\star) &= \lambda m-\log Z = r, \\ D(R\|Q^\star) &= -bm-\log Z = E.
\end{align*}
Therefore,
\begin{equation*}
m=r-E=\mu,
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation}
\log Z = \lambda\mu-r = -b\mu-E.
\label{eq:polynomial-order-saddle-identities}
\end{equation}

The projection inequalities at the selected order give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K_P(\lambda) &:= \log\mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda h}]
\leq \log Z, &&P\in\mathcal P, \\ K_Q(-b) &:=\log\mathbb E_Q[e^{-bh}] \leq \log Z, &&Q\in\mathcal Q.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:polynomial-order-projection-envelopes}
\end{equation}

For $P\in\mathcal P$, define the tilted distribution
\begin{equation*}
\widehat P(x) := P(x) \exp\!\left\{\lambda h(x)-K_P(\lambda) \right\}.
\end{equation*}
The family
\begin{equation*}
\left\{ \widehat P:P\in\mathcal P \right\}
\end{equation*}
is compact and uniformly positive. Moreover, $h$ is nonconstant. Indeed, if $h$ were constant, then $Q^\star/P^\star$ would be constant on the common support. Since both laws have unit mass, this would imply
$P^\star=Q^\star$, contradicting
\begin{equation*}
r<D(\mathcal Q\|\mathcal P).
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-finite-range-weighted-residuals} therefore applies uniformly to this tilted family.

For any $t\in\mathbb R$, change of measure gives
\begin{align*}
P^n\{S_n\geq t\}&=e^{nK_P(\lambda)-\lambda t} \\[-0.25ex] &\quad\times \mathbb E_{\widehat P^n}\!\left[e^{-\lambda(S_n-t)}\mathbf 1\{S_n\geq t\} \right].
\end{align*}
Taking $t=n\mu+\delta$, applying
\eqref{eq:uniform-right-weighted-residual}, and using \eqref{eq:polynomial-order-saddle-identities} and \eqref{eq:polynomial-order-projection-envelopes} yield
\begin{equation}
P^n\{S_n\geq n\mu+\delta\} \leq C_{\mathcal P} n^{-1/2} e^{-nr-\lambda\delta},
\label{eq:polynomial-order-null-tail}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathcal P}$ is independent of $P$. Similarly, for $Q\in\mathcal Q$, define
\begin{equation*}
\widehat Q(x):=Q(x)\exp\!\left\{-bh(x)-K_Q(-b)
\right\}.
\end{equation*}
The corresponding tilted family is also compact and uniformly positive. For any $t\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{align*}
Q^n\{S_n<t\} &= e^{nK_Q(-b)+bt} \\[-0.25ex] &\quad\times \mathbb E_{\widehat Q^n}\!\left[ e^{b(S_n-t)} \mathbf 1\{S_n<t\}\right].
\end{align*}
Taking $t=n\mu+\delta$ and applying \eqref{eq:uniform-left-weighted-residual} gives
\begin{equation}
Q^n\{S_n<n\mu+\delta\} \leq C_{\mathcal Q} n^{-1/2} e^{-nE+b\delta},
\label{eq:polynomial-order-alternative-tail}
\end{equation}
uniformly over $Q\in\mathcal Q$. Set
\begin{equation*}
\delta_n:=-\frac{\log n}{2\lambda}+B.
\end{equation*}
By \eqref{eq:polynomial-order-null-tail},
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P} \mathbb E_{P^n} \left[ \varphi_n^{(B)} \right] \leq C_{\mathcal P} e^{-\lambda B} e^{-nr}.
\end{equation*}
Choose $B$ such that
\begin{equation*}
C_{\mathcal P}e^{-\lambda B}\leq1.
\end{equation*}
Then $\varphi_n^{(B)}\in\Phi_n(r;\mathcal P)$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Equation~\eqref{eq:polynomial-order-alternative-tail} gives
\begin{align*}
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q} \mathbb E_{Q^n} \left[ 1-\varphi_n^{(B)} \right] &\leq C_{\mathcal Q} e^{bB} n^{-1/2-b/(2\lambda)} e^{-nE} \\ &= C_{\mathcal Q} e^{bB} n^{-1/(2\lambda)} e^{-nE}.
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{eq:polynomial-order-projected-upper} and the upper bound in \eqref{eq:composite-polynomial-order}. We next prove the matching lower bound. Under $R^n$, define
\begin{equation*}
T_n:=S_n-n\mu.
\end{equation*}
The likelihood ratios and
\eqref{eq:polynomial-order-saddle-identities} give, for every event $A$,
\begin{align}
(P^\star)^n(A) &= e^{-nr} \mathbb E_{R^n}\!\left[
e^{-\lambda T_n}\mathbf 1_A \right],\nonumber \\ (Q^\star)^n(A) &= e^{-nE} \mathbb E_{R^n}\!\left[ e^{bT_n}\mathbf 1_A \right].
\label{eq:selected-pair-exact-change-of-measure}
\end{align}
The distribution $R$ has full support,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_R[h]=\mu,
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Var}_R(h)>0.
\end{equation*}
The two sided bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-finite-range-weighted-residuals} therefore imply, uniformly for $\delta_n=O(\log n)$,
\begin{align}
(P^\star)^n\{T_n\geq\delta_n\} &= \Theta\!\left( e^{-nr} n^{-1/2} e^{-\lambda\delta_n}\right),
\label{eq:selected-null-polynomial-window} 
\\ (Q^\star)^n\{T_n<\delta_n\} &= \Theta\!\left( e^{-nE} n^{-1/2} e^{b\delta_n}\right). 
\label{eq:selected-alternative-polynomial-window}
\end{align}
The same orders hold with strict and weak endpoints interchanged. Let
\begin{equation*}
d_n := -\frac{\log n}{2\lambda}.
\end{equation*}
Using the lower and upper constants in \eqref{eq:selected-null-polynomial-window}, choose fixed constants $B_-<B_+$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{align}
(P^\star)^n \{T_n\geq d_n+B_-\} &> e^{-nr}, \nonumber \\ (P^\star)^n \{T_n>d_n+B_+\} &< e^{-nr}.
\label{eq:selected-np-threshold-bracket}
\end{align}
By the NP theorem \cite[Th.~3.2.1, pp.~60--61]{lehmann2005testing}, an optimal randomised test with Type I error exactly $e^{-nr}$ has the form
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\varphi_n^{\rm NP} &= \mathbf 1\{T_n>t_n\} \\ &\quad+ \eta_n\mathbf 1\{T_n=t_n\}, \quad 0\leq\eta_n\leq1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The inequalities in \eqref{eq:selected-np-threshold-bracket} imply that
\begin{equation*}
d_n+B_- \leq t_n \leq d_n+B_+.
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
(Q^\star)^n \{T_n<d_n+B_-\} &\leq \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P^\star,Q^\star)
\\ &\leq (Q^\star)^n\{T_n\leq d_n+B_+\}.
\end{align*} 
Equation~\eqref{eq:selected-alternative-polynomial-window}, including its weak endpoint version, now gives \eqref{eq:selected-pair-polynomial-order}. Since the corresponding threshold for $S_n$ is $n\mu+t_n$, it also gives \eqref{eq:selected-pair-polynomial-threshold}.
Finally, every test admissible for the composite null is admissible under $P^\star$, and its worst case Type II error is no smaller than its Type II error under $Q^\star$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q) \geq \beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star (r;P^\star,Q^\star).
\end{equation*}
Combining this inequality with \eqref{eq:selected-pair-polynomial-order} proves the lower bound in \eqref{eq:composite-polynomial-order}. Taking logarithms gives \eqref{eq:composite-polynomial-logarithmic-refinement}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the polynomial refinement]
\label{rem:composite-polynomial-order-scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:composite-polynomial-order} is an eventual
$\Theta$ relation. It does not provide a ratio one expansion, a universal leading constant, or an optimal finite blocklength bound for every $n$. Lattice effects and boundary randomisation may change bounded multiplicative factors without changing the polynomial order. The theorem also does not imply that $(P_r^\star,Q_r^\star)$ is least favourable at finite blocklength.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consistency of the saddle order]
\label{rem:composite-polynomial-saddle-order-consistency}

If two saddle triples furnished by
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-composite-hoeffding-saddle} had orders
$\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, applying
\eqref{eq:composite-polynomial-order} to both triples would make the same
sequence comparable to both
$n^{-1/(2\lambda_1)}e^{-nE}$ and
$n^{-1/(2\lambda_2)}e^{-nE}$. Hence $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$.
Pair uniqueness is neither asserted nor required.
\end{remark}

\section{Stochastic monotonicity in a natural exponential family}
\label{app:nef-stochastic-monotonicity}

\begin{lemma}[Stochastic monotonicity]
\label{lem:nef-stochastic-monotonicity}

Let $\{P_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:one-parameter-nef}. For every $n\geq1$, every $\theta_1<\theta_2$, and every bounded measurable nondecreasing function $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{P_{\theta_1}^n}[f(T_n)]
\leq \mathbb E_{P_{\theta_2}^n}[f(T_n)],
\label{eq:nef-stochastic-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
T_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n T(X_i).
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For $\theta_2>\theta_1$,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dP_{\theta_2}^n}{dP_{\theta_1}^n} =\exp\left\{(\theta_2-\theta_1)T_n-n\bigl[\psi(\theta_2)-\psi(\theta_1)\bigr]
\right\}=:L(T_n).
\end{equation*}
The function $L$ is nondecreasing and
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{P_{\theta_1}^n}[L(T_n)]=1.
\end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{align*}
&\mathbb E_{P_{\theta_2}^n}[f(T_n)]-\mathbb E_{P_{\theta_1}^n}[f(T_n)]\\
&\qquad= \operatorname{Cov}_{P_{\theta_1}^n}
\bigl(f(T_n),L(T_n)\bigr).
\end{align*}

Let $U$ and $U'$ be independent copies of $T_n$ under $P_{\theta_1}^n$. Then
\begin{align*}
2\operatorname{Cov} \bigl(f(U),L(U)\bigr) = \mathbb E\Big[ \bigl(f(U)-f(U')\bigr) \bigl(L(U)-L(U')\bigr) \Big].
\end{align*}
Both $f$ and $L$ are nondecreasing, so the integrand is nonnegative. This gives \eqref{eq:nef-stochastic-monotonicity}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\section{Numerical illustrations of ordered exact reduction}
\label{app:ordered-family-numerical-checks}

Figures~\ref{fig:ordered-subcritical} and
\ref{fig:ordered-supercritical} illustrate the ordered endpoint result for
the Bernoulli, Poisson, Gaussian, and exponential families. In each example,
monotone likelihood ratio ordering identifies the adjacent endpoint pair
$(P_{\rm e},Q_{\rm e})$ and gives
\begin{equation*}
\beta_n^\star(r;\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
=
\beta_{n,\mathrm{sp}}^\star
(r;P_{\rm e},Q_{\rm e})
\end{equation*}
for every blocklength and rate. The minimax curves can therefore be computed
from the corresponding simple binary endpoint problems. These figures
illustrate exact reduction for the ordered families and do not imply the
same reduction for general nonordered classes.
